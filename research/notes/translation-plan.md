# Translation plan

Written 2026-10-20. This covers the texts that need a fresh CC BY 4.0
translation because no free English translation exists. For each one: the
base text, where to get it, the copyright concerns, and the order of work.
Copyright findings are in `copyright.md`.

## Ground rules

1. **Translate from the original-language text.** A modern edition is the
   source for the *readings* (the transcribed ancient text). Under US law,
   facts and the ancient text itself are not copyrightable. An editor's
   restorations of lacunae, notes and apparatus may be.
2. **Mark restorations.** Where the translation follows an editor's
   restoration of a gap, put it in square brackets and credit the editor in a
   note ("restored by Allberry"). Don't reproduce the apparatus.
3. **Copyrighted translations are a cross-check, never a source.** Draft
   first, then compare for errors, and never adopt their wording wholesale.
4. **Use public-domain facsimiles where possible.** Photographs of ancient
   manuscripts (2-D works) are not copyrightable in the US
   (*Bridgeman v. Corel*, 1999).
5. **Every translation page records** its base edition (with page and line),
   the translator ("Manichaean Scripture Compendium"), the date, and how much
   has been checked.

## Practical points

- **Languages:** Chinese, Coptic, Greek and Arabic can be done at reasonable
  quality, with every passage flagged for expert review. The Middle Iranian
  and Old Uyghur texts are harder. Lean on the public-domain German of Müller
  and Le Coq, and say so on the page.
- **Review state** on every page: *draft* (machine-assisted first pass),
  *checked* (compared against the original line by line), or *reviewed*
  (checked by a named reader). Everything starts as *draft*.
