## Introduction

This is one of the longest Manichaean accounts of the making of the world
to survive in an Iranian language. It comes from two leaves of a
Middle Persian book found at Turfan. A god, who is not named in what
survives but is the Living Spirit (the demiurge), binds the powers of
darkness in the sky, builds the sun and moon from the light that has been
purified out of the mixture, and sets up the layered heavens and earths,
with their walls, gates, pillars, ditches and prisons, to hold the demons
and to drain the light upwards. The blue running heads, which belong with
the text that follows them, read "To me, the child of Jesus" and "The
writing of the new and the …".

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's first
edition of 1904, the only public-domain edition, which he called "a first
attempt". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense. Where Müller notes that the
scribe corrected a word, the correction is followed silently.

## The translation

### M 98, first page. Blue heading: "To me, the child of Jesus"

[ … ] he fastened the seven planets, and he bound and fettered the two
dragons, and tied them up above on that lowest heaven; and so that they
should turn (it) at the call, he charged two angels above, a male and a
female.

Then he led them up to the border and the height of the light. And from
wind and light, water and fire, which had been purified out of the
mixture, he made two vehicles (?) of light: that of the sun, of fire and
light, with five walls, of ether, wind, light, water and fire, and twelve
gates, five houses, three thrones and five soul-gathering angels, in the
fiery wall; and that (vehicle) of the moon-god, of wind and water, with
five walls, of ether, wind, light, fire and water, and fourteen gates,
five houses, three thrones and five soul-gathering angels, in the watery
wall. These he made and set in order. He clothed them within.

### M 98, second page. Blue heading: "The writing of the new and the …"

Then he clothed the sun-god, from the same purifying, in three garments,
of wind, water and fire; and what had been purified (out) sank (?) down to
the earth of darkness.

And to praise above the great mystery (?), the new Paradise, he filled (?)
those five [ … ] of death and made them whole (?); and, corresponding (?)
to the heavens, he drew out (?) and laid down (?) above the earth of
darkness four deposits (?), one deadly and dark, one fiery, one watery,
one above the other.

And he raised one wall along (the edge of) the earth of light, to the
east, the south below and the west, and joined it back (?) to the earth
of light. He made another great earth, and laid it down over the deposits;
and over it he made the *Mānbed* (the house-guardian) the commanding god.
And on that same earth, within, (he made) another wall, beyond (?) the
east, south and west, and in those same three regions three pillars and
five crowns (?) (the rest is destroyed).

### M 99, third page. Blue heading: "Which is the least in splendour"

[ … ] the west to the western pillar; and the second from the western
pillar to the southern pillar; and the third from the southern pillar to
the eastern pillar; and the fourth from the eastern pillar to the wall,
the beginning, which is in the east; and the fifth, the great one, from
the east to the western pillar.

And (he made) an earth, great and firm, with twelve gates that correspond
(?) to the gates of the heavens; and on that same earth, round about, he
made four walls and three ditches; and in the innermost ditch he fastened
(?) the demons.

And (he set) the lowest heaven above its head, and so that his hand might
keep the heaven in order round about from there, he set up seven pillars
within; and Mānbed the god laid down that great earth upon the pillars and
the crowns (?) and the two walls; then (?) [ … ] to the east, the south
(the rest is destroyed).

### M 99, fourth page. Blue heading: "And all the …"

[ … ] and to the west, over the furthest wall, and to the north, in the
earth of light, he set (it) in order. And over that great earth (he made)
the *mazaman* earth (?); and below the ditches, two other mixed earths.
The door of all [ … ] he made, to lead up much wind, water [and fire].

And round the earth he set in order one wall with four gates. And in the
four regions he set four angels, who hold up the lowest heaven,
corresponding (?) to and dressed like those above.

And to tear away (?) the darkness of the four regions, he made twelve
hells, three in each region. And below them, round about, (he made) a
rampart (?) because of the sea; and on that same rampart (?) he set up a
prison because of the risings (?) of the *mazan*s.

Then over the face of the earth, mountains [ … ] (the rest is destroyed).

## Notes to this edition

- **The two vehicles** (M 98): Müller compares Augustine, *On Heresies*
  46: the Manichaeans say the moon was made of good water and the sun of
  good fire. Here the sun is of fire and light and the moon of wind and
  water.
- **"Soul-gathering angels"**: Müller compares Bar Hebraeus, who reports
  that the Good One made two great ships in the sky, the sun and the moon,
  in which he gathers the souls of men.
- **The heavens and earths** (M 99): Müller compares al-Nadīm: "He built
  ten heavens and eight earths, and charged one angel to carry the heavens
  and another to hold up the earths." The four angels who hold the lowest
  heaven correspond to these.
- **The two last leaves** of the same quire contain a hymn in praise of
  Mani in couplets, which Müller only sampled: "A messenger came from the
  Paradise of light, of shining name, the chosen one, the god Mār Mānī."
- ***Mānbed*** (M 98–99): "master of the house"; Müller compares Avestan
  *nmānapaiti*. The same god appears in the *Šābuhragān* fragments.
- ***mazan*** (M 99): giant demons; Müller writes "the Mazanian ones",
  probably after Mazandaran, the land of demons in Iranian legend.
