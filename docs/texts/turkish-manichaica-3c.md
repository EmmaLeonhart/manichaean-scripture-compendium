## Introduction

These pieces belong to Le Coq's group of colophons and leaves naming
historical persons. The most important (no. 15, T M 417) is a blessing
spoken by the Manichaean community of Qočo when a new *Iduq-qut*, the
Uyghur ruler of Qočo, took the throne after the death of his predecessor.
Lines of it had already been printed by F. W. K. Müller in *Uigurica*
(1908). The other pieces are a short hymn on a god who came down from
heaven and brought a *mahistak* named Noydar, a blessing on a *možak* and
his community, and fragments of sermons.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German, which he
called "first attempts at interpretation". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Le
Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in parentheses are added for
sense. The numbering of the smaller pieces follows the order in which Le
Coq printed them.

## The translation

### No. 15 (T M 417): the blessing on the new Iduq-qut

*A large leaf, 28.5 × 17 cm, in Manichaean script; the top line and the
upper inner edge destroyed. Found at Qočo.*

**Front.** [ … ] shining. Because he has deigned (to set) like the sun-god,
in the capital [ … ] first of all the whole two shining communities [ … ],
the youths (?) (and) the Elect; outside in the province, the nobles living
in the blessed land, the princesses, the god-like princes, the Il-ügäs, the
envoys and all those of name; his people, high and low, who act like birds
[ … ] like dogs, cattle and two-footed ones (?); from the blue heaven above
down to the king of the earth-god: we, the souls and spirits, rejoicing
with all our heart, have reverently wished for this day of fortune. Because
the majesty of Il-Ötükän has deigned to give strength, because we have all
reverently found the whole happiness of the heart, therefore, my divine
one, from the heart, with loving mind and great love, we reverently speak
blessings and ask for joy!

The bestowing of the title, which we have performed, with the divine,
illustrious name on our divine king, the Iduq-qut; his gracious taking of
his seat on the golden throne, on the jewelled throne; his becoming
majestic and fortunate: may it come to pass! May the four shining kings of
the gods come, the burxans mighty in prayer who are in the ten directions!

**Back.** [ … ] the souls who have been set free [ … ], having completed
their own affairs, have gone to the realm of the gods. "That (throne?)
shall not remain empty," saying [ … ]; so he deigned to sit on that
throne. All the burxans, strong in prayer, as if of one face (?), changing
name and form, appeared to save (set free) the souls.

When our prince the Iduq-qut deigned (to set) like the sun-god, we, the
whole poor, wholly common people, were grieved and troubled. As, in the
place of the sun-god, the shining moon-god deigns to appear shining, so our
prince the Iduq-qut deigns to appear in the place of Awlawč. It was (his)
pleasure (the command) to sit down on the golden throne.

May the princely majesty of Il-Ötükän, the majesty of the former wise
prince-fathers and of the princes, the majesty of this illustrious throne,
settle on our divine prince the Iduq-qut! From the seven and the twelve
mighty rulers may strength and help come; may fair, longed-for, many-
coloured light and splendour settle on the majesty of our brave, virtuous,
high and mighty prince! And on his whole kingdom and all its order [ … ]

## Notes to this edition

- **The Iduq-qut** (no. 15) was the title of the Uyghur rulers of Qočo.
  The blessing compares the death of the former ruler with the setting of
  the sun and the new ruler with the moon that shines in its place;
  *Awlawč* is apparently the name of the predecessor. Il-Ötükän is the
  sacred homeland of the Turks, here personified as a "majesty".
- **"The seven and the twelve"** (no. 15) are the planets and the signs of
  the zodiac, here as the "mighty rulers" whose favour is wished on the
  prince.
- **Noydar the *mahistak***: a church official of the third rank (the
  *mahistag*, "elder"); the god *Tvai* is not identified (Le Coq's reading is
  uncertain).
