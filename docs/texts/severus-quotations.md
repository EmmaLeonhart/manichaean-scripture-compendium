## Introduction

Severus, patriarch of Antioch (512–518), devoted his 123rd Cathedral
Homily to refuting the Manichaeans, and to do so he quoted at length "one
of the books (of Mani) of which one should not speak", which another
version calls "one of his secret books". The Greek is lost, but the homily
survives in two Syriac translations, by Paul of Callinicum and by Jacob of
Edessa. Kugener and Cumont edited the Syriac and translated it into French
in 1912. The quotations are Mani's own words in their oldest form outside
the Coptic and Iranian texts: the two eternal principles pictured as the
Tree of Life and the Tree of Death, the Tree of Death divided against
itself, and the rising of the powers of darkness to the border of the land
of light, which made the "mixture" necessary.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Kugener and Cumont's
French, which follows Jacob of Edessa's version; their notes on Paul's
variant readings are not reproduced. Only the quotations of Mani are given;
Severus' commentary between them is summarized in italics.

## The translation

*Severus: "He says, in one of his books which one should not speak of, or
rather which are worthy of darkness and error, the following":*

### The Tree of Life

As for the things that exist perpetually and at all times from the
beginning, he (Mani) speaks of Matter and of God, each of them existing by
its nature. So exists the Tree of Life, which is adorned there with all its
beauties and splendours, which is filled and clothed with all its goods,
and (which is) firm and stable in its nature.

Its land holds three regions: that of the North, which is outside and
below, and the East and the West, (which are likewise) outside and below.
And below, there is nothing that is plunged or clothed by it in any of the
regions; but the infinite is outside and below. There is no foreign body
around or below the infinite, nor in any place of these three regions; but
it (the Tree of Life) is of itself below and outside, to the North, the
East and the West. And nothing surrounds it or encloses it in these three
regions, but it is in itself, of itself, enclosing in itself its fruits;
and the kingdom is in it.

*Severus says that Mani then adds, "after many endless follies", about the
good (Tree):*

It is not seen in the southern region, and that because it is hidden in
what is in its bosom. For God has surrounded this place with a wall.

*And he adds other things:*

Its light and its goodness (of the Tree of Life, or the good Tree) are not
visible, so that it may not give an occasion of desire to the Evil Tree
which is in the South, and may not be for it an occasion of being excited,
tormented and put in danger. But it is enclosed in the glory, and gives no
occasion, because of its goodness. It has protected itself in its justice,
and it is in this glory, while being continually in the nature of its
greatness in the three regions.

### The Tree of Death

And the Tree of Death has no life by its nature, and has no fruits of
goodness on any of its branches. And it is continually in the southern
region. And it has also a place of its own, (namely) that which is above
it.

*Severus: "First they say this about the same Matter":*

The Tree of Death is divided into a great number (of trees); war and
cruelty are in them; they are strangers to peace, filled with complete
wickedness, and never have good fruits. It (the Tree of Death) is divided
against its fruits, and the fruits (are divided) against the Tree; they are
not united to the one who begot them, but all produce the moth for the
corruption of their place; they are not subject to the one who begot them,
but the whole Tree is evil; it never does anything good, but it is divided
against itself, and each of its parts corrupts what is near it.

*Severus notes that these are a series of extracts set side by side rather
than one continuous passage.*

### The rising of the members of darkness

*Severus: "They have also written these violent things":*

These things concern Matter, its fruits and its members. But the occasion
of rising up to the worlds of Light was given them by their rebellion. For
these members of the Tree of Death did not know one another and had no
notion of one another. For each of them knew nothing more than its own
voice, and they saw (only) what was before their eyes. When one (of them)
cried out, they heard (it). They perceived that, and they rushed with
violence towards the voice. They knew nothing else. So they were stirred
and driven on by one another to go as far as the borders of the glorious
land of Light. When they saw the sight (of the Light), wonderful and
splendid, which is far above their own, then they gathered together, that
is, the dark Matter, and they plotted against the Light, so as to mix with
it. They did not know, in their folly, that a powerful and strong God
dwelt there. So they sought to climb and to rise, because they had never
heard of the divinity, or had (never) understood who it was.

[ … ] (they were seized) with desire for the sight of those blessed worlds,
and they thought that it would become theirs. All the [ … ] corrupting
(Matter) rose up, then, and climbed with countless powers, whose number
cannot be told. All were clothed with the matter of fire. And these
members were different. For some had hard bodies and were of infinite
size; the others, bodiless and intangible, had a subtle tangibility, like
the demons and the spectres of phantoms. After rising, the whole of Matter
climbed, with its winds, its storms, its waters, its demons, its phantoms,
its princes and its powers, all searching carefully how they might get into
the Light.

### Why the mixture was needed

Because of the agitation that was stirred up from the depths against the
Land of Light and against the holy fruits, it was necessary that a portion
of the Light should come and mix with these Evil Ones, so that the enemies
might be caught by means of the mixture, that there might be peace for the
Good, that the nature of the Good might be preserved, [ … ] that, on the
other hand, the Shining Ones might be rid of Matter by the power that had
been mixed (with it), so that Matter might be destroyed and the Tree of Life
might become God in all and over all. For in the world of Light there is no
burning fire that could be hurled against Evil, there is no cutting iron,
there are no choking waters, nor any other such evil thing. For all there
is light and noble region, and it cannot harm it; but there is this way
out, that is, this means, so that the enemies, scattered by the portion
broken off from the Light, may stop their onrush and be caught by means of
the mixture.

*Severus' homily goes on to refute these passages at length, quoting some of
them again; those repetitions are not given here.*

## Notes to this edition

- **Which book?** Severus does not name it; Paul's version calls it "one of
  his secret books". The list of Mani's books is on [a separate
  page](al-nadim-books.html).
- **Three regions**: the Tree of Life fills the North, East and West, and is
  limited only on the South, where the Tree of Death is. Severus mocks the
  idea that the infinite God should be bounded and need a wall.
- **"The moth"** (Greek *sēs*): the fruits of the Tree of Death breed the
  moth that destroys them, as moths destroy wool.
- **Compare** the same myth in [al-Nadīm](al-nadim-teaching.html) (the two
  principles and the rising of Satan to the border of the light) and in the
  [Fundamental Epistle](fundamental-epistle.html) (the Father sends forth a
  deity against the darkness).
