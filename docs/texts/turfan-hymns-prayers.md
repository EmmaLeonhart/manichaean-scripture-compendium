## Introduction

Besides the longer leaves, Müller's 1904 edition prints many small pieces
of hymns, prayers and stories. This page gathers some of the most
important: a hymn to Mani with a meditation on death (M 383); Mani's own
song, from a little liturgical book, "I am the first stranger, the son of
the god Zurwān" ("M. liturg."); a line of a hymn of the soul (M 442); a
scene from the mission of Mār Ammō, who took the faith to Khorasan (M 2);
the Manichaean *Sanctus*, with the Hebrew *qādōš*, "holy" (M 75, M 331);
and a prayer for an Uyghur khan (M 43).

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's first
edition of 1904, the only public-domain edition, which he called "a first
attempt". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense.

## The translation

### M 383: a hymn to Mani, and a meditation

*The remains of a small leaf.*

[ … ] and before [ … ] let us praise, [ … ] let us praise, [ … ] together
(?). Mani, lord of fair name; Mani, lord of fair name, fair, lovely
[ … ]

**Back.** [ … ] the [ … ].

*In red, a heading, partly lost.*

I know where I was born,
and I do not know where I shall die;
for I have enjoyed in the body [ … ],
for I have given in the soul [ … ] (the rest is lost).

### "M. liturg.": Mani's own song

*A small book, a kind of liturgy, damaged at the top and bottom edges. It
contains this passage.*

*In red: "This in his own song".*

I am the first stranger,
the son of the god Zurwān,
the child of the ruler.

*(The same text then follows again, drawn out syllable by syllable for
chanting.)*

*Müller adds in his corrections three related verses from other leaves.
From the same small book, page 1:*

I am a man, a seed (?) of light, living and shining.

*From its back:*

I am a ruler's son [ … and] I have become a stranger, (far) from the
Greatness.

*From M 7:*

From the light and from the gods am I,  
and I have become a stranger to them.  
The enemies have fallen (?) upon me,  
and by them I have been led down (?) to the dead.

### M 442: a line of a hymn

*A fragment with two and a half double lines each side. It begins:*

For I am the soul of the faith [ … ]

### M 2: from the story of Mār Ammō

*A large double leaf, almost complete. Page 3, column 2. The two halves of
the column are read in order.*

[ … ] then the spirit of the frontier of Khorasan appeared, in the form of
a maiden. She asked me: "What are you doing? Where have you come from?" I
said: "I am a believer, a disciple of Mani the Apostle."

That spirit said: "I do not receive you. Turn back to where you have come
from." And she hid herself (vanished) from before me. Then I, Ammō, stayed
for two days in praises before the sun. Then the angel appeared and said:
"Do not be faint-hearted [ … ]"

### M 75: the Sanctus

*About half a page. Heading: "Bless[ing]".*

[Holy!] to your Greatness, all-shining (?), most high, [and to your]
radiant beauty, which is imperishable. Holy! Holy! Holy! Holy! Holy! Holy!
to your praised Kingdom. Holy! Holy! Holy! to you, Father! Holy! to your
chosen Name! Holy! Holy! Holy! to you, Father! Holy! Holy! Holy! [ … ]

**Back.** *Heading: "[ … ]-udagān".*

To your great Thought, from which every thought and good deed (came).
Holy! Holy! Holy! (to your Word), from which every word and good deed, and
the Strongest, came. Holy! Holy! Holy! to you, Father! Holy! To your great
and praised Air, which is above every air, for in the kingdoms [ … ] (the
rest is destroyed).

### M 331: the Sanctus (another copy)

*A badly preserved piece of a larger leaf.*

[Holy!] [ … to your] Kingdom [ … ] Holy! Holy! Holy! to your [ … ] Holy! to
your chosen Name! Holy! Holy! [to you,] Father! Holy! Holy! Holy! [to your]
Light [and your] Power, Father, who shine above all. Holy! Holy! [Holy!] to
you, Father! Holy! to you [ … ] from which all splendour [came]. Holy!
Holy! [Holy!] to you, Father! Holy! to you [ … ] (the rest is destroyed).

**Back.** *(Müller gave no German for this side. It contains the words
"all praise", "all life", "of the shining ones", "the rulers", "king",
"praise", "for ever and ever", and the refrain "you are, you are, you
are".)*

### M 43: a prayer for an Uyghur khan

*A small oblong leaf; the writing is rubbed away in places.*

[ … the religion … of] Khorasan, the holder of the throne, *Tängrimiz
Xan* (Turkish: "our divine khan"). May they keep it always, for ever, with
the seed (the line) of the shining ones. Live well and glad! Dwell in joy
and welfare! Live for ever!

The stronger, the warlike, the mighty god, praised in prayer, first (?)
commander of the heroes, seeker of battle, excellent, Jacob the great
angel; [and] the mighty ones, the glories, the spirits:

**Back.** May they bless you, ruler, famous holder of the throne, wearer
of the diadem, *Tängri Xan Kül Bilgä Xan* (a Turkish name), so that new
bliss may increase for you, [new] joy and new gladness, help (?) and new
victory may come from every side. May the gods, the shining ones and the
angels be your watchers and protectors; may they gladden you all your
days. May your throne grow strong (?) in joy for ever (?), for long years;
may you always remain glad! (end of the fragment).

## Notes to this edition

- **"I know where I was born, and I do not know where I shall die"**
  (M 383): a common saying on the uncertainty of death, here in a
  Manichaean hymn.
- **"The first stranger"** ("M. liturg."): Müller compares Matthew 25:43,
  "I was a stranger and you did not take me in", which the *Šābuhragān*
  quotes in the same words (*uzdeh*), and John 1:11–12. **Zurwān** is the
  Persian name the Manichaeans gave to the Father of Greatness.
- **"From the light and from the gods am I"** (M 7): Müller compares the
  apocryphal Gospel of Philip quoted by Epiphanius: "I am from those
  above".
- **Mār Ammō** (M 2) was the disciple Mani sent to Abarshahr and Khorasan.
  Müller headed the column "from the story of Mār Ammō". The spirit who
  bars the way is the spirit of the frontier of Khorasan.
- **The Sanctus** (M 75, M 331): Müller notes that *qādōš* is certainly the
  Hebrew word, as in Isaiah 6:3, and that other hymns have "Say: Holy!
  Holy!" and "Amen! Amen!" The Greatness, Light, Power and the rest are the
  "majesties" of the Father.
- **The khan** (M 43): Müller read the Turkish titles *Tängrimiz Xan* and
  *Tängri Xan Kül Bilgä Xan*. The Uyghur khans made Manichaeism their
  state religion from 762.
