## Introduction

The *Sermon of the Light-Nous* survives in Chinese as the
[Traité](traite.html), and parts of it in Parthian and Sogdian. Le Coq
found that a number of Old Turkish leaves from Qočo, from four different
books, contain another version of it. He placed them against the pages of
Chavannes and Pelliot's edition of the Chinese, and noted that the Turkish
often differs a good deal from the Chinese and is meant for the Elect. One
leaf (T M 423d) in the same book has instead part of a lively Iranian
legend, so the book was probably a collection of important writings for the
Elect.

The section numbers in the headings below refer to the sections of the
Traité on this site.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German, which he
called "a first attempt at interpretation". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words
Le Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in parentheses are added
for sense.

## Notes to this edition

- **The parallels.** Le Coq matched T II D 119 with Chavannes and Pelliot's
  pages 541–545 (the Traité's §10, on the five limbs Sense, Mind, Thought,
  Reflection and Intention and the five virtues that grow from them); T M
  300 with pp. 547–548 (§§8–9); T M 423c and b with pp. 567–571 (§§13–14,
  the days and nights in the body and the twelve kingships); the leaf from ruin α with
  p. 578 (the leaf from ruin α), and T M 423a with pp. 583–584 (§15, the signs of the Elect).
- **"Thirteenth, the god of the majesty of the Law"** (T II D 119): in the
  Chinese the five lights and the twelve virtues are summed up in the same
  way, "together they make thirteen pure bodies of light" (§13).
- **The four books.** The leaves come from four different books found at
  different places in Qočo; Le Coq took this as proof that the text was
  important or popular.
- **T M 423d**: *Xorug* is not identified; the "Kögmän mountains" are
  probably the Sayan range of Turkic tradition. Le Coq calls the piece an
  Iranian legend; the speaker seems to be a winged being.
