## Introduction

The *Acts of Archelaus* is a fourth-century Christian work, written in Greek
by one Hegemonius and surviving whole only in Latin. It tells how Mani
wrote to a rich Christian, Marcellus, of the city of Carchar in Roman
Mesopotamia, and came to debate with its bishop, Archelaus. The story is
fiction, but the work preserves two Manichaean pieces. The first is a letter
that opens "Manichaeus, apostle of Jesus Christ", the formula Mani used at
the head of his letters, and is presented as Mani's own. The second is the
report of Mani's former disciple Turbo, who sets out "the faith of Mani"
point by point; its contents agree closely with the Manichaean texts found
since. Epiphanius copied both into his *Panarion* in the original Greek,
so the Latin can be checked against it.

The *Acts* are not on Wikipedia's list of Manichaean texts that this
compendium follows, so this page is filed as related material.

**This translation is new and is a draft.** It is made from the Latin of
Beeson's edition (1906), read from an OCR scan, with the Greek of
Epiphanius printed beside it consulted where the Latin is unclear. Words in
parentheses are added for sense.

## The translation

### The letter to Marcellus (chapter 5)

Manichaeus, apostle of Jesus Christ, and all the saints and virgins who are
with me, to Marcellus, my dearest son: grace, mercy and peace from God the
Father and our Lord Jesus Christ; and may the right hand of light keep you
from this present evil age, and from its ruins and the snares of the evil
one. Amen.

Feeling your boundless love, I rejoiced greatly; but that your faith is not
according to right reason grieved me. So, since I have been sent to set
right the human race, and to help those who have given themselves up to
seductions and errors, I have thought it necessary to send you this
writing: first for the salvation of your own soul, and then of those who
are with you, so that you may not have a mind that makes no distinctions,
as the teachers of the simpler folk teach, saying that evil and good come
from the same author, and bringing in a single beginning, and not
examining or distinguishing the darkness from the light, or the good from
the evil, or the outer man from the inner, as we said before, but never
ceasing to confuse and mix the one with the other.

But you, my son, do not, like most men, mix both together without reason,
simply and without distinction, and do not insult the God of goodness. For
they refer the beginning and the end, and the father of these evils, to
God, whose end is close to a curse. For they do not believe what is said in
the Gospels of our Saviour and Lord Jesus Christ, that a bad tree cannot
bear good fruit, nor a good tree bad fruit. How they dare to call God the
maker and creator of Satan and of his evil deeds, I greatly wonder. And
would that their folly had gone no further than this, and that they did
not say that the only-begotten Christ, who came down from the bosom of the
Father, was the son of a certain woman, Mary, begotten of blood and flesh
and the other filth of women!

And so as not to write much in this letter and draw out your patience at
length, since I have no natural eloquence, it is enough to have said this.
You will learn everything when I am with you, if indeed you are eager to
spare and look after your salvation; for I cast a noose on no one, as most
of the foolish do. Understand what I say, honoured son.

### Turbo's account of Mani's teaching (chapters 7–9)

*Archelaus and Marcellus ask Turbo, who has brought the letter from Mani,
to tell what Mani teaches. Turbo answers:*

If you wish to learn from me the faith of Mani, hear it briefly. He
worships two gods, unbegotten, existing of themselves, eternal, the one
opposed to the other; and he brings in the one as good and the other as
evil. To the one he gives the name Light, and to the other Darkness. And
(he says) that the soul which is in men is part of the light, and the body,
and the creation that comes from matter, of the darkness. He says that the
mixture or union came about in this way, likening the two gods to an
example of this kind:

[It is] as if two kings were fighting each other, who had been enemies
from the beginning, each having his own portion; and it happened that the
darkness went forth from its bounds and joined battle with the light.

When the good Father learned that the darkness had come upon his land, he
brought forth from himself a power, which is called the Mother of Life,
and with it he surrounded the First Man; these are the five elements, that
is, wind, light, water, fire and matter. Clothed with these, as if with the
equipment of war, he went down to fight against the darkness. But the
princes of the darkness, fighting against him, ate of his armour, which is
the soul. Then the First Man, our first parent, was grievously afflicted
down there by the darkness; and had not the Father heard him as he prayed,
and sent another power which had gone forth from himself, which is called
the Living Spirit, and had it not come down and given him its right hand
and led him out of the darkness, the First Man would long ago have been held
fast and in peril. So he left the soul down there. And this is why the
Manichaeans, when they meet one another, give each other the right hand, as
a sign of this, as men set free from the darkness; for all heresy, he says,
is in darkness.

Then the Living Spirit made the world; and clothed with three other powers,
he went down, brought up the princes and crucified them in the firmament,
which is their body, the sphere. And again the Living Spirit himself made
the lights, which are the remains of the soul, and made them go round the
firmament; and again he made the earth, and there are eight (earths). And
the Omophoros is below, that is, the one who carries it on his shoulders;
and when he grows tired of carrying, he trembles, and this is the cause of
earthquakes out of their appointed time. For this reason the good Father
sent his son from his bosom into the heart of the earth and its inner
parts, to restrain him as was fitting; for whenever there is an earthquake,
it is caused by his trembling from fatigue, or by his shifting the weight
from one shoulder to the other.

Then Matter too made plants (or shoots) out of itself; and when these were
stolen by certain princes, it called together all the chief princes, took
from each of them a single power, and made this man after the likeness of
that First Man, and bound the soul in him. This is the basis of the mixture.

When the living Father saw the soul afflicted in the body, because he is
compassionate and merciful, he sent his beloved son for the salvation of the
soul; for this reason, and because of the Omophoros, he sent him. And the
son came and changed himself into the likeness of a man; he appeared to men
as a man, though he was not a man, and men thought he had been born.

When he had come, he set up a device put together for the salvation of
souls, that is, a wheel, with twelve buckets, which turns with this sphere
and draws up the souls of the dying; and the greater light, that is, the
sun, takes them with its rays, purifies them and hands them to the moon, and
so the disc of the moon, as we call it, is filled. For he says that these
two lights are ships or ferry-boats; and when the moon is full, it ferries
the souls across to the eastern region, and so makes its waning, when it is
relieved of its load; and the boats are filled again and unloaded again,
while the souls are drawn up by the buckets, until it has set free its own
part of the souls. He holds that every soul, and every living thing that
moves, takes its part from the substance of the good Father. So when the
moon has handed over the load of souls it carries to the aeons of the
Father, they remain in the Column of Glory, which is called the Perfect
Man. This Man is a column of light, for it is filled with pure souls; and
this is the cause of the salvation of souls.

The cause of death for men is this. A certain Maiden, fair and adorned and
very graceful, by stealth draws on the princes who are in the firmament,
who were brought up and crucified by the Living Spirit. When she appears,
to the males she appears as a fair woman, to the females she shows herself
as a handsome and desirable young man. And the princes, when they see her
adorned, are moved to lust by love of her; and because they cannot seize
her, they are violently driven on, kindled by the fires of love, for they
are carried away by the heat of desire. So when, as they run after her, the
maiden suddenly vanishes, then that great prince brings forth clouds from
himself, to darken the whole world in his anger; and when he has been
greatly troubled, as a man sweats after toil, so this prince too sweats from
his trouble, and his sweat is the rain. And the prince of the harvest (of
pestilence), too, if he has been deceived by the maiden, pours out famine
(plague) over the whole earth, so as to bring death on men. For this body is
called a world, after the likeness of this great world; and all men who are
below have their roots bound to those above. So when (that prince) has been
deceived by the maiden, then he begins to cut the roots of men; and when
their roots are cut, then pestilence begins, and so they die. And if he
shakes the upper parts of the root, an earthquake comes and follows, as the
Omophoros is shaken too. This is the cause of death.

## Notes to this edition

- **The opening of the letter** follows the form of Paul's letters, as
  Mani's letters did; the Turfan Middle Persian Gospel fragment has the same
  self-description, "I, Mani, the apostle of Jesus the Friend" ([Mani's
  Gospel and Epistles](turfan-gospel-epistles.html)).
- **"The right hand of light"**: the giving of the right hand among
  Manichaeans, explained by Turbo as a memory of the Living Spirit giving
  his right hand to the First Man, is found in the Coptic and Iranian texts
  too.
- **"The prince of the harvest"**: the Latin translator read the Greek
  *limos* ("famine") where Epiphanius has *loimos* ("plague"), so the Latin
  has "famine"; both are given.
- **Turbo's account goes on** (chapters 10–13) to the transmigration of
  souls, the rules for the Elect and the hearers, the prayers, and the end
  of the world; those chapters are not yet translated here.
- **The wheel with twelve buckets, the ships of light, the Column of Glory
  and the seducing Maiden** are the same images as in the Coptic, Iranian
  and Chinese texts; compare [Theodore bar Konai](theodore-bar-konai.html)
  and the [Turfan cosmogony](turfan-cosmogony.html).
