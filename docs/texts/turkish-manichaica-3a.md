## Introduction

These are the first seven groups of texts in Le Coq's last volume of Old
Turkish Manichaica (1922). Most are small and damaged, and Le Coq called
all his translations "first attempts at interpretation". No. 6 comes from
the same book of sermons as the leaves T II D 173 in his first volume (see
[Old Turkish texts from Qočo](turkish-manichaica-1.html)); no. 7 from the
same book as T II D 176.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German. Gaps are
marked [ … ]; words Le Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in
parentheses are added for sense. Where Le Coq gave no German for a leaf,
that is said.

## Notes to this edition

- **Siin** (no. 1): Le Coq could not decide whether this name of a god is
  the Babylonian moon-god Sin or a Chinese word.
- **The moon's waxing and waning** (no. 2): in Manichaean teaching the moon
  fills with the light gathered from the world and empties it into the sun
  each month.
- **The demon under the mountains** (no. 3): the Living Spirit
  (*Wadžiwanta*) holds the defeated demons down under the earth and the
  mountains, with Mount Sumeru on the trunk.
- **Mšiha Burxan** (no. 6) is the Messiah, Jesus. "One loaf … one vessel of
  your water" recalls Matthew 10:42 ("a cup of cold water").
- **The question about the moon-god and Zurwān** (no. 6) shows that the
  Great Gospel (Mani's Living Gospel) was read in Turkish communities.
- **Kanig Rōšan** (no. 7) is the Maiden of Light, here identified with
  "wise knowledge"; Yišō is Jesus, and Wahman Rōšan the Light-Nous.
