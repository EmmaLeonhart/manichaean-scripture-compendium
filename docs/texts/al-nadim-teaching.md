## Introduction

Al-Nadīm's chapter on the Manichaeans (987) gives a long summary of Mani's
teaching, much of it introduced as Mani's own words: "Mani teaches …",
"Mani says further …". It is the fullest account of the Manichaean myth
that survives in Arabic, and it agrees closely with the Turfan fragments
(compare [the Šābuhragān](shabuhragan.html) and [the building of the
world](turfan-cosmogony.html)). This page gives the part on the two
principles, the battle of the First Man, the mixture, the making of the
world and the end; the making of Adam and Eve and the story of their
children; the lands of light and of darkness; and how a person should enter
the religion. Al-Nadīm's lists of Mani's books and letters, and the
law Mani gave, are on [a separate page](al-nadim-books.html).

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Gustav Flügel's German of
1862. Flügel's notes are not reproduced. Words in parentheses are his or are
added for sense.

## The translation

### The two principles

Mani teaches: The beginning of the world is two beings, the one light, the
other darkness; the two are separate from each other.

The light is the first Great One, not limited by any number, God himself,
the King of the Paradises of Light. He has five limbs: gentleness,
knowledge, understanding, the mystery and insight; and five others,
spiritual: love, faith, faithfulness, nobility and wisdom. He held further
that the God of Light, with these his attributes, is without beginning, but
that with him two things exist that are equally without beginning, the one
the air (the ether of light), the other the earth. Mani adds: The limbs of
the air are five, gentleness, knowledge, understanding, the mystery and
insight; and the limbs of the earth are the gentle breeze, the wind, the
light, the water and the fire.

The other being is the darkness, and its limbs are five: the mist, the
burning, the scorching wind, the poison and the darkness.

Mani teaches: That being of light borders directly on the dark being, with
no wall between them; the light touches the darkness with its (lowest) side,
while upwards, to the right and to the left it is without limit. In the
same way the darkness is endless downwards, to the right and to the left.

### Satan and the First Man

Mani teaches further: From this dark earth Satan arose; not that he was
eternal in himself from the beginning, but his substances were without
beginning in his elements. These substances from his elements came
together and came forth as Satan: his head like the head of a lion, his
body like the body of a dragon, his wings like the wings of a bird, his
tail like the tail of a great fish, and his four feet like the feet of
creeping things.

When this Satan, under the name of Iblīs, the eternal (in time) first devil,
had arisen out of the darkness, he swallowed and devoured (everything),
spread ruin to right and left, and went down into the depth, bringing
destruction and annihilation from above in all these movements. Then he
strove upwards and caught sight of the rays of the light; but they were
hateful to him. When later he saw how they rose higher, he was terrified,
shrank into himself and united with his elements. Then he strove upwards
again with such force that the earth of light became aware of Satan's doing
and how he was bent on murder and ruin. When it had learned of it, the
world of insight learned of it, then the world of knowledge, then the world
of the mystery, then the world of understanding, then the world of
gentleness.

When at last, he teaches further, the King of the Paradises of Light had
also learned of it, he thought of how to subdue him; and those hosts of his,
he adds, would have been strong enough to overcome him, but he wished to do
it by his own power. So he brought forth, with the spirit of his right hand,
his five worlds and his twelve elements, a creature, and that is the
eternal (in time) Man (the First Man), and called him to fight the darkness.

The First Man, Mani says further, armed himself with the five kinds, and
these are the five gods: the gentle breeze, the wind, the light, the water
and the fire. He made them his armour, and the first that he put on was the
gentle breeze. Then he wrapped the exalted gentle breeze in the burning
light as in a cloak, drew over the light the water full of atoms, and
covered himself with the blowing wind. Then he took the fire in his hand as
a shield and a lance, and rushed swiftly down from the Paradises, until he
came to the border of the region that touches the battlefield.

The first devil, too, took his five kinds, namely the smoke, the burning,
the darkness, the scorching wind and the mist, armed himself with them,
made them a shield for himself, and went out against the First Man. After
they had fought a long time, the first devil overcame the First Man,
swallowed some of his light, and at the same time surrounded him with his
kinds and his elements.

Then the King of the Paradises of Light followed him with other gods,
freed him and overcame the darkness. The one with whom the King of Light
followed the First Man is called the Friend of the Lights. He came down,
and the First Man was freed from the hellish substances, together with what
he had seized and hidden of the spirits of darkness.

Then, Mani goes on, Joy and the Living Spirit set out for the border; they
looked down into the abyss of this deep hell, and saw the First Man and the
angels, how Iblīs, the overbearing oppressors and the dark life surrounded
them. And the Living Spirit, Mani says, called the First Man with a loud
voice, as swift as lightning, and the First Man became another god.

### The mixture

When the first devil, Mani teaches further, had entangled the First Man in
the battle, (parts) of the five parts of the light mixed with the five parts
of the darkness. The smoke mixed with the gentle breeze, and from this comes
the (present) air: so that what is pleasant and refreshing in it for the
soul and for animal life comes from the gentle breeze, and what is harmful
and ruinous in it comes from the smoke. The burning mixed with the fire:
so what is burning, destroying and ruinous in fire comes from the burning,
but what gives brightness and light in it comes from the fire. The light
mixed with the darkness: so what is in it of these thick bodies, such as
gold and silver and the like, and further what is in it of clearness,
beauty, purity and other useful things, comes from the light, and what is
in it of dirt, impurity, thickness and hardness comes from the darkness.
The scorching wind mixed with the wind: what is useful and pleasant in the
wind comes from the wind, and what is oppressive, wounding and harmful in
it comes from the scorching wind. The mist, lastly, mixed with the water:
so that what is clear, sweet and agreeable to souls in water comes from the
water, and what is sinking, choking and destroying in it, its heaviness and
corruption, comes from the mist.

When, Mani says further, the five dark kinds had been mixed with the five
shining kinds, the First Man went down to the lowest depth of the abyss,
and cut off the roots of the five dark kinds, so that they should not grow.
Then he went back up to his place on the battlefield. Then, he goes on, he
commanded one of the angels to draw this mixture after him towards the
side of the earth of darkness that borders on the earth of light, and they
hung it up on high. Then he set another angel over it and handed over to
him these mixed parts.

### The building of the world

And the King of the World of Light, Mani teaches, commanded one of his
angels to make the present world and to build it from these mixed parts, so
as to free those parts of light from the dark parts. So he built ten
heavens and eight earths, and charged one angel with carrying the heavens
and another with holding the earths up on high. To each heaven he gave
twelve gates with their porches, great and wide. Each of the gates stood
opposite its keeper, and on its opposite side, at each of the porches, were
two folding doors. In these porches he made, at each of their gates, six
steps, and on each of the steps thirty passages, and in each passage twelve
rows (places); and he laid out the steps, the passages and the rows from
the highest porch upwards, in the height of the heavens.

He joined, Mani says further, the air at the lowest of the earths with the
heavens; he made a ditch round this world, to throw into it the darkness
that he would separate from the light; and behind this ditch he raised a
wall, so that nothing of this darkness, once separated from the light,
should escape.

### The sun and moon, and the Column of Praise

Mani goes on: Then he made the sun and the moon, to purify out what light
there is in the world: so that the sun purifies the light that was mixed
with the hot devils, and the moon the light that was mixed with the cold
devils, at the Column of Praise; and the light so purified rises up,
together with the praises, hymns, the pure word and the pious works that
rise up. The moon, Mani adds, hands these parts of light over to the sun,
and the sun hands them over to the light above it in the world of praise,
and they go on in this world up to the highest, pure light. She does not
cease doing this until there remains of the light a part so closely bound
that the sun and the moon cannot purify it any further.

### The great fire and the end

While this is happening, the angel whose task is to carry the earths rises
up, and the other angel ceases to draw the heavens after him, so that the
highest mixes with the lowest; and a fire blazes up and eats its way
through these confused things, and does not stop burning until what is
still left of light in them is released.

This burning, Mani teaches, lasts 1,468 years. And, he goes on, when this
order has been carried through, and the Humāma, that is, the spirit of
darkness, sees the freeing of the light and the rising up of the angels,
the hosts and the keepers, she humbles herself as she sees the battle, so
that the hosts round about her press upon her, and she goes back into a
grave that has been made ready for her. Then he closes this grave with a
stone as big as the world, and shuts her in. The light is then safe from
the darkness and from any harm by it.

The Māsiya among the Manichaeans hold that something of the light remains
behind in the darkness.

### The beginning of unbroken generation, according to Mani's teaching

Then, Mani says, one of those archons and of the stars, the oppressing
force, greed, lust and sin, mated, and from their mating came forth the
first man, who is Adam; and those who watched over him were two archons, a
male and a female. Then followed a second mating, and from it came forth
the beautiful woman, who is Ḥawwāʾ (Eve).

When, Mani teaches, the five angels saw the light of God, which greed had
secretly stolen and shut up captive in these two creatures, in its
defilement, they begged the bringer of good news, the Mother of Life, the
First Man and the Living Spirit to send someone to this first creature, to
loosen and save it, to reveal knowledge and righteousness to it, and to set
it free from the devils. So, he goes on, they sent ʿĪsā (Jesus), with a god
accompanying him. These seized the two archons, made them prisoners and
freed the two creatures.

And ʿĪsā (Jesus), Mani teaches, set out and spoke to the creature, that is,
Adam; he explained to him the Paradises and the gods, hell and the devils,
the earth and the heaven, and the sun and the moon; he made him afraid of
Ḥawwāʾ by telling him of her violent importunity, and filled him with fear
of going near her. And Adam obeyed.

Then the archon went back to his daughter Ḥawwāʾ (Eve) and lay with her in
the lust that dwells in him, so that she bore a son, ugly to look at and
reddish, named Qāyin (Cain), the red man. This son then lay with his
mother, and she bore a white son named Hābīl (Abel), the white man. Then
Qāyin lay with his mother again, so that she bore two girls, the one named
the Wise of the World (*Ḥakīmat ad-dahr*), the other the Daughter of Greed
(*Ibnat al-ḥirṣ*). Qāyin took the Daughter of Greed as his wife and gave the
Wise of the World to Hābīl, who took her as his wife. The Wise of the
World, Mani says, overflowed with the light and wisdom of God, while in the
Daughter of Greed there was none of it at all.

Then one of the angels came to the Wise of the World and said to her:
"Guard your soul, for to God's joy you shall become the mother of two
perfect girls." And he lay with her, so that she bore him two girls, one of
whom she named Raufaryād ("Come to help") and the other Barfaryād ("Bring
help"). When Hābīl learned of this, he was filled with anger, and sadness
took hold of him wholly, and he said to her: "From whom have you these two
children? I suspect they are Qāyin's; it is he who has lain with you." She,
however, described to him the form of the angel. Still he left her, went to
his mother Ḥawwāʾ (Eve) to complain to her of what Qāyin had done, and said
to her: "Have you heard what he has done with my sister and my wife?"
Qāyin heard this, fell upon Hābīl, smashed his brain with a stone and killed
him. Then he took the Wise of the World as his wife.

Mani teaches further: Those archons, that Ṣindīd (the mighty one), and
Ḥawwāʾ were grieved at what they saw of Qāyin; and the Ṣindīd taught Ḥawwāʾ
the gibberish of sorcery, so that she might bewitch Adam. She went off, did
as she was told, and met him with a garland of tree blossom. When Adam found
her fit for his lust, he lay with her, and she became pregnant by him and
bore a graceful son, fair of face. The Ṣindīd heard of this, fell into
grief and became ill, and said to Ḥawwāʾ: "This creature does not belong to
us; it is a stranger." She wished to kill it; but Adam took it and said to
Ḥawwāʾ: "I will feed it with cow's milk and the fruit of the trees." He
took it and went away. Then the Ṣindīd sent the archons to take away the
trees and the cattle and remove them far from Adam.

When Adam saw this, he took the child and drew three circles round it. On
the first he wrote the name of the King of the Paradises, on the second the
name of the First Man, and on the third the name of the Living Spirit; he
went up to a higher place, humbled himself before God, whose name is
glorious, and begged him: "If I have sinned against you, that is not the
fault of this child." Then one of the three came hurrying, bringing the
garland of beauty, which he carried in his hand to Adam. When the Ṣindīd
and the archons saw this, they went straight away.

Then, Mani says, a tree called *Lūṭis* (lotus) appeared to Adam, from which
milk came out. With this he fed the boy, and gave him the name of the tree.
Later he called him Šātil (Seth). But that Ṣindīd, who kept up his enmity
against Adam and those creatures, said to Ḥawwāʾ: "Go to Adam; perhaps you
will bring him back to us." She hurried off at once and deceived Adam, so
that he lay with her in his lust. When Šātil saw him then, he admonished
him, reproached him and said to him: "Come, let us set out for the East,
to the light and wisdom of God." And he set out with him there, and he
stayed there until he died and entered Paradise. But Šātil, Raufaryād and
Barfaryād, and the Wise of the World, the mother of the last two, followed
with the *ṣiddīqūt* (the community of the righteous) one direction and one
and the same way until the time of their death; while Ḥawwāʾ, Qāyin and the
Daughter of Greed went into hell.

### The earth of light and the ether of light, which are without beginning, together with the God of Light

Mani teaches: The earth of light has five limbs, the gentle breeze, the
wind, the light, the water and the fire; and likewise the ether of light
has five limbs, gentleness, knowledge, understanding, the mystery and
insight. He teaches further: These ten limbs of the ether and the earth
together make up the Greatness.

This shining earth, he says, has a body; it is bright and serene, with a
gentle radiance and full splendour, against which the clearness of its
purity and the beauty of its bodies stand out: form upon form, beauty upon
beauty, whiteness upon whiteness, clearness upon clearness, grace upon
grace, light upon light, brilliance upon brilliance, lovely sight upon
lovely sight, pleasantness upon pleasantness, elegance upon elegance, rows
of gates upon rows of gates, towers upon towers, dwellings upon dwellings,
inns upon inns, gardens upon gardens, trees upon trees, branches upon
branches with boughs and fruit, lovely to see, a light shining in many
colours, each lovelier and more blooming than the other, clouds upon clouds
and shade upon shade. That God of Light on this earth is an eternal God.

The God on this earth, he goes on, has twelve glories, which are called
the first-born, whose forms are like his form, all of them learned and
understanding; and glories, he says, which are called the house-spirits,
the active and the strong. And the gentle breeze, he adds, is the life of
the world.

### The earth of darkness and its heat

The earth of darkness, Mani teaches, bristles with depths, abysses, clefts,
layers, dams, swamps, scattered stretches of land running out into branches
and filled with dense forest, and springs from which smoke rises from land
to land and from wall to wall, from which fire comes up from land to land
and darkness from land to land. Some of these columns are higher than the
others, others deeper, and the smoke that pours out of them is the poison
of death. It rises from the spring of an abyss whose foundations are of
murky slime, in which are dusty earth, the elements of fire, the thick dark
elements of wind and the elements of heavy water.

The darkness borders on this shining earth, above (the one) and below (the
other). Both, the light upwards and the darkness downwards, are without
limit.

### How a man should enter the religion

Whoever wants to enter the religion, Mani commands, must test himself; and
if he sees that he is able to tame lust and greed, to give up eating all
kinds of meat, drinking wine and married intercourse, and to keep himself
from harming water and fire, from sorcery and from hypocrisy, let him enter
the religion. But if he is not able to do all this, let him not enter. If
he loves the religion but cannot subdue the stirring of the senses and
greed, let him make use of keeping the religion and the righteous (the
Elect), and meet his reprehensible acts by (using) times when he gives
himself wholly to work, righteousness, eager watchfulness, prayer and pious
humility; for that makes him content in this passing world and in the
eternal world to come, and his form on the Last Day will be the second
form, of which, God willing, we shall speak further below.

## Notes to this edition

- **The five limbs** of the Father (gentleness, knowledge, understanding,
  mystery, insight) are the same five that the Chinese texts call Sense,
  Mind, Thought, Reflection and Intention (see the [Traité](traite.html)).
- **The Friend of the Lights, Joy and the Living Spirit**: the gods of the
  second creation who rescue the First Man. "Joy" (Flügel: *die
  Fröhlichkeit*) is usually taken to be the Mother of Life; Le Coq compared
  the Turkish "Mother-God" who comes down with the Living Spirit (see [Old
  Turkish texts from Qočo](turkish-manichaica-1.html)).
- **Ten heavens and eight earths**: Müller compared this passage with the
  Turfan cosmogony (see the notes to [the building of the
  world](turfan-cosmogony.html)).
- **1,468 years**: the same figure as the Middle Persian *Šābuhragān*
  (M 470).
- **Adam, Eve and their children**: Jesus is sent to wake Adam; Šātil
  (Seth), fed from the milk of the lotus tree, leads Adam to the East and is
  the ancestor of the righteous, while Eve, Cain and the Daughter of Greed
  go to hell. The *Ṣindīd* is the chief of the archons who made the first
  pair.
- **"The second form"** (entering the religion): the hearer who cannot live
  as an Elect will have the "second" rank at the Last Day; al-Nadīm promises
  to explain it later in his chapter.
- **The Humāma** is the "spirit of darkness", the soul of matter.
