## Introduction

M 4 is two double leaves (eight pages) of a Manichaean hymnbook from
Turfan. Red rubrics tell the cantor which hymn to have sung next ("Let him
have sung the hymn of …") and mark where each cycle ends. Most hymns are
in Parthian; the prayers to the angels at the end invoke Jacob, Raphael,
Michael, Gabriel and Sarael, and ask blessing on "the whole church of the
province of Khorasan".

The best-known piece is a hymn spoken in Mani's own voice: "I have come
forth from the land of Babel … to call a call in the world." Müller set
beside it al-Bīrūnī's report of Mani's words about himself, and a Mandaean
hymn of the Envoy of Light: "A call have I raised in the world."

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's first
edition of 1904, the only public-domain edition, which he called "a first
attempt". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense. Rubrics are given in
italics.

## Notes to this edition

- **"Let him have sung"** (Müller: *nisārād*, "er lasse rezitieren")
  introduces each hymn; "ended is" (*hanzaft*) closes it. The hymns are
  grouped in cycles: the Last Days (*frašegird*), Body and Soul, and the
  Going Out (of the soul at death).
- **Mani's hymn of Babel** (page 2): Müller compares Ephrem's "Mani from
  Babel", Mani's own statement reported by al-Bīrūnī (Sachau's edition of
  the *Chronology*, p. 208), and a Mandaean hymn: "A call have I raised in
  the world; in the world have I raised a call."
- **"Come, souls, to this ship of light"** (page 3): Müller compares the
  passage of Mani's *Treasure* quoted by Augustine, *On the Nature of the
  Good* 44: the washed souls go up to the ships of light "prepared for
  their carrying over to their homeland".
- **"Where you were from the beginning"**: Müller compares al-Nadīm: the
  gods lead the dead "to the place where he was at first in the Paradise
  of light".
- **Frēdōn, Narimān, Jacob** (pages 5, 6 and 8) are heroes of Iranian
  legend and a patriarch, taken over here as angels or guardian figures.
  **Zurwān** is the Manichaean name, in Persian, for the Father of
  Greatness. **Bar Simus** is not identified.
