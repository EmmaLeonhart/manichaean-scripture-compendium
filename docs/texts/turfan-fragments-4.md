## Introduction

The last group of Middle Persian pieces in Müller's 1904 edition. M 48 and
M 566, in the same hand, tell of the Apostle (Mani) carrying a man called
Ardāv up into the air, and of Mani before a king as "a physician from the
land of Babel"; Müller compared al-Bīrūnī's report that Mani rose into the
air with King Shapur. A leaf of a conversion story lists the evils of
anger, killing and wine, and tells how a righteous man prayed for the soul
of Dārāv's son. M 45 belongs to a story about a hearer called Khebrā. M 64
is a hymn written out a second time, syllable by syllable, for chanting.
M 16 is from a teaching on the fast days; M 28 is polemic against the
fire-worshippers and the Christians; M 50 is a calendar table.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's first
edition of 1904, the only public-domain edition, which he called "a first
attempt". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense.

## The translation

### M 48: the Apostle and Ardāv

*A small piece of a double leaf. From a legend.*

[ … ] (whereupon) the Apostle led Ardāv into the air (?). He said: "What is
higher?" Ardāv said: "My [ … ] (sphere?)." The Apostle said: "[What is]
greater?" He said: "The earth, which bears everything." Again he asked:
"Of these, what is greater?" Ardāv said: "The heaven [ … ]"

**Back.** [ … ] kingdoms came (?), the righteous and the sinner. And the
deeds of Ardāv and [ … ]. Then, when the nobles of the king of Tūrān heard
this word, they were glad; they accepted the faith, and became [followers]
of the Apostle and of the religion. Again, when the king of Tūrān [ … ]
(end).

### M 566: "a physician from the land of Babel"

*By the handwriting, from the same text as M 48. Only this survives:*

"[ … ] I have come before the king. Welfare [ … ] from the gods." The king
said: "Where are you from?" I said: "I am a physician from the land of
Bab[el]."

**Back.** [ … ] came over the whole body [ … ] the girl became well. In
great joy she said to me: "Where are you from, you, my god and
life-giver (?)?" [ … ] (end of the leaf).

*From the other leaf that belonged with it only a few letters and words
survive, among them: "Then Mār Mā[nī]".*

### A conversion story: the evils of anger and wine

*A leaf fragment, from a conversion story. (Müller's number for it cannot
be read in the scan.)*

[ … ] Second, that he becomes [ … ]. Third, that he becomes violent.
Fourth, that the soul becomes filthy. Fifth, that greed increases. Sixth,
that he becomes overbearing (?). And seventh, that he falls into much (bad
company?). Eighth, that the preaching (?) is not heeded (?). Ninth, that his
honours remain without good works. Tenth, that sin arises through cooking.
Eleventh, that understanding fades (?). Twelfth, that he leads others (?)
to kill.

And of harming there are nine kinds: first, that he kills himself; second,
that he leads others to it; third, that he does not drink; fourth, that he
eats; fifth, that he gives to eat; sixth, that [ … ] teaching; seventh, that
he sells; eighth, that he has others buy; and ninth, (left blank in the
manuscript).

From wine and drunkenness come nine kinds of harm and sin. First, that he
loses his senses. Second, that he becomes sad. Third, that he becomes
remorseful. Fourth, that he [ … ]. Fifth, that fear arises from falling.
[Sixth … ] punishment [ … ]. Eighth, that he becomes shameless [ … ]

**Back.** [ … ] to Khebrā, that the son of Dārāv may find salvation [ … ]
saw a sign. And in those days the righteous man had come. They made the
reading (preaching?) before him. And at the time when the righteous man
prayed in the blessing for this singer, he did reverence three times at the
word (?). And the sons said: "Show us why you did reverence." He said: "I
did reverence to Jesus, the Father of insight and Lord, for he is my guide.
From him I asked, and the prayer that you prayed he has also accepted. And
truly (?) the angels brought the soul of Dārāv and set it before me,
adorned in the wonderful robe of rulers." And when Abursām and Khebrā
heard, they went and fell at the righteous man's feet and said: "[ … ] to
you, Lord, and free from oppression [ … ] I will save the soul, and to you
[ … ] because they have seen [ … ]" (end of the fragment).

### M 45: Khebrā the hearer

*A leaf fragment, from a story. Heading: "Khebrā the hearer".*

[ … ] are set in order. Then that woman too, who remembers the girl,
(said) that "this woman, who has a son, has made all these arrangements,
and I have one only daughter; now all that this woman has done and
arranged, I too must do in the same way." And she wrote [ … ]

**Back.** *Red heading: "On …".*

"I, who until now did not know that when I weep over the bodily son, I
kill (strike) the spiritual one; but from now on I will not weep, I will
not kill him." He asked forgiveness of sins and kindness. When he [ … ]
(the rest is lost).

### M 64: a song

*Red heading in the Parthian "dialect": "This [ … ] the callers".*

Mār [Mānī] has come from the gods, the god of shining fame; to Paradise
[he leads up?] [ … ] where the wind brings a lovely scent.

*(The same text follows, drawn out for chanting, with "the immortal" in red
between the lines. A red note follows: "In the same song …".)*

**Back.** The pure god has come from the Paradise of light, the good one,
the word (?) [ … ]

*(Again drawn out for chanting. Then in red:)* "This the song of the
friends answers."

*(Little can be made out of the other four lines of the leaf.)*

### M 16: on the fast days

*The lower half of a leaf. From a teaching.*

[ … ] that he keeps that [ … ], and as I have taught you this freedom of
these two fast days, and so also at the tens (?) of N., so he gives proof
that in the month of Tīr (June) there is one (such) day, so that everyone
who knows it and keeps the fast on it in purity becomes spotless of great
defilement.

**Back.** [ … ] is, and the name [ … ] the great day; he, N., (as) shown in
my (?) book; because a great and strong work is made firm on those
fourteen days. And in another book of the Zoroastrians he writes thus: on
the fourteen days of the month of Tīr, a deed to the doer [ … ] (end of
the fragment).

### M 28: against the fire-worshippers and the Christians

*A small, badly preserved double leaf in very small writing; the text on
pages 3 and 4 is in groups of four or five lines. Müller printed samples.*

**From page 3.** [ … ] and also these, who worship the burning fire: from
this they can know for themselves that their end (will be) in the fire.
And they say that Ohrmizd and Ahriman are brothers, and because of this
word they come to destruction (?) [ … ]

They call on the "Son of Mary", the son of Adonai, the seventh [ … ]. If
that one is Lord of the All, his son, who made [ … ] They, the wicked,
come rightly to hell, for by themselves sinfulness was made, and the
destruction (?) of the sinners is like what they have done. He, the god of
Marcion, [ … ] took his own [ … ] and killed [ … ]

**From page 4.** Then at the end (the Last Day) all who worship idols will
come, on that last day, and go to destruction (?).

### M 50: a calendar table

*A badly damaged larger leaf, worm-eaten, in black and red, in a late,
very cursive hand. A table belonging to a chronological or calendrical
work.*

One column gives the names of the Persian days of the month: Srōš,
Mahraspand, Rašn, Dēn, Anērān, Frawardīn, Ard, Wahrām, Āštād, Rām, Asmān,
Wād, Zāmyād. Beside Zāmyād is written in red: "[ … ] of Mercury and Mars"
(in Arabic). A column on the back gives, in Arabic but in Manichaean
script, the signs of the zodiac: Lion, Virgin (the "ear of corn"), Balance,
Scorpion, Bow(man), Goat, Water-carrier, Fishes. The other columns are
filled with letters and notes in a very cursive hand.

## Notes to this edition

- **Ardāv and the Apostle** (M 48): the word *frēstag* means both "angel"
  and "apostle". Müller suggests that the Apostle of Light, Mani, is meant,
  and compares al-Bīrūnī: "the king Shapur came to believe in him when he
  had ascended with him towards heaven, and they had been standing in the
  air between heaven and earth".
- **"A physician from the land of Babel"** (M 566): compare Mani's hymn of
  Babel in [the hymnbook M 4](turfan-hymnbook-m4.html), and the king's
  remark about "this art of medicine" in [M 3](turfan-mani-legends.html).
- **"When I weep over the bodily son, I kill the spiritual one"** (M 45):
  Müller compares the Zoroastrian ban on mourning the dead with tears.
- **Ohrmizd and Ahriman as brothers** (M 28): the Zurvanite teaching, which
  Müller compares with Eznik of Kołb, *Against the Sects*.
- **The god of Marcion** (M 28): Marcion of Sinope taught that the god of
  the Old Testament was a different, lesser god from the Father of Jesus.
- **The Parthian "dialect" texts**: Müller's edition goes on to print
  samples in what he called a "Pehlevi dialect" (Parthian), with Turkish
  and trilingual leaves (M 172). Those are on the queue to be done next.
