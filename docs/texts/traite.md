## Introduction

This text is called the *Traité* after the title of its first edition,
Chavannes and Pelliot's "Un traité manichéen retrouvé en Chine" (1911), and
the *Sermon of the Light-Nous* after its subject. It survives in one Chinese
manuscript from Dunhuang, now in Beijing, which lacks its opening lines. The
Taishō canon prints it under the misleading title 波斯教殘經, "Fragmentary
Scripture of the Persian Religion". Parts of the same sermon survive in
Parthian and Sogdian, which shows that it was a Manichaean work translated
across the church's whole range.

In the dialogue, the Envoy of Light (明使) answers his disciple Adda (阿馱),
the apostle who took the faith to the Roman East. Adda asks how the light
bound in the body can be freed. The Envoy explains that the Demon of Greed
built the human body as a small copy of the universe, to be a prison for
the light, and that the Light-Nous (惠明, "Wise Light") comes into the
person to free it. The Light-Nous overthrows the "old man" and builds the
"new man", cutting down the five dead trees of the passions and planting
five living trees of virtue. The last part lists the twelve signs by which
a true member of the Elect can be recognised.

## The translation

### 1. Adda's question

[…] if they do not meet with the right conditions, they have no means of
freeing themselves; seeking release […] the original nature of the body of
flesh: is it one, or is it two? All the holy ones who appear in the world
use skilful means and are able to save the light-nature, so that it leaves
all suffering behind and comes at last to peace and joy.

When he had asked this, he bowed low in reverence and stepped back to stand
to one side.

### 2. The building of the world as a prison for darkness

Then the Envoy of Light said to Adda: "Excellent, excellent! For the good of
countless beings you have been able to ask about so deep and secret a
meaning. You are now a great good friend to all the blind and lost beings of
the world. I will explain it to you part by part, so that the net of your
doubts is cut away for ever and nothing is left of it.

"Know this. Before this world was set up, the two Envoys of Light, the
Living Spirit and the Good Mother, went down into the dark pit, the realm of
ignorance. They drew up the valiant one, [the First Thought, with his]
armour of great wisdom, the fivefold body of light, and led him upward, and
brought him out of the five pits. But the five kinds of demons stuck to the
five bodies of light like flies stuck in honey, like birds caught with
lime, like fish that have swallowed the hook.

"For this reason the Living Spirit, Envoy of Light, took the two forces
mingled together, the five kinds of demons and the five bodies of light,
and made the world out of them: ten heavens and eight earths. So this world
is the medicine-hall where the body of light is healed, and also the prison
where the demons of darkness are held in chains.

"The Living Spirit and the Good Mother, with skilful means, set up the ten
heavens. Next they placed the wheel of deeds and the palaces of the sun and
moon, and below them the eight earths, the three garments, the three
wheels, and even the three calamities, the iron enclosure, the four walls,
Mount Sumeru (未勞俱孚山) and all the lesser mountains, the great sea and the
rivers. When they had made all this, they had built the world and shut up
the five kinds of demons. All of them are held prisoner by the thirteen
great powers of light.

"These thirteen great valiant powers are the five sons of light of the
First Thought and the five of the Living Spirit, together with Xrōštag and
Padvāxtag, and the Srōšahrāy. The five bodies of light are like the
prison, and the five kinds of demons like its prisoners. The five sons of
the Living Spirit are like the prison officers. The Call and the Answer (說聽,
喚應) are like the night watchmen calling the hours. The thirteenth, the
Srōšahrāy, is like the king who judges cases.

### 3. The Demon of Greed builds the human body as a copy of the world

"When the Demon of Greed saw this, she laid an evil plan once more in her
poisoned heart. She made Luxian (路㑥) and Yeluojue (業羅決) copy the Living
Spirit and the Good Mother, and by transformation among themselves they
built the human body. They imprisoned the light-nature in it and modelled
it on the great world. This poisonous, evil body of greed and lust, small
as it is, copies every part: heaven and earth, the world, the wheel of deeds
and the stars, the three calamities, the four walls, the great sea and the
rivers, the dry and the wet earths, plants and trees, birds and beasts,
mountains and streams, hills and mounds, spring, summer, autumn and winter,
years, months, hours and days, and even the bounded and the unbounded.
There is nothing in it that does not copy the world. It is like a goldsmith
who copies the shape of a white elephant and engraves it inside a ring: the
elephant's body is neither larger nor smaller (than the original). The human
world is the same.

"The Living Spirit took the five kinds of demons and imprisoned and bound
them in the thirteen pure bodies of light, and did not let them be free.
When the Demon saw this, she conceived a greedy and poisonous heart. She
imprisoned the five light-natures in the body of flesh as a small world,
and bound it fast with the thirteen dark powers of ignorance, and did not
let it be free.

### 4. The five prisons and the five dead trees

"The Demon of Greed imprisoned the pure Air in the city of bone, set dark
Sense there, and planted a dead tree. She imprisoned the wondrous Wind in
the city of sinew, set the dark Mind there, and planted a dead tree. She
imprisoned the Light in the city of the veins, set dark Thought there, and
planted a dead tree. She imprisoned the wondrous Water in the city of flesh,
set dark Reflection there, and planted a dead tree. She imprisoned the
wondrous Fire in the city of skin, set dark Intention there, and planted a
dead tree.

"The Demon of Greed planted these five poisonous dead trees in five kinds
of ruined ground. With them she always confuses the original nature of the
light, and draws out that stranger-nature (客性) and turns it into
poisoned fruit. The tree of dark Sense grows in the city of bone, and its
fruit is resentment. The tree of the dark Mind grows in the city of sinew,
and its fruit is anger. The tree of dark Thought grows in the city of the
veins, and its fruit is lust. The tree of dark Reflection grows in the city
of flesh, and its fruit is wrath. The tree of dark Intention grows in the
city of skin, and its fruit is folly.

"So the five, bone, sinew, veins, flesh and skin, are made into prisons to
hold the fivefold body, just as the five lights (of the cosmos) hold the
demons prisoner. And resentment, anger, lust, wrath and folly are made the
prison officers, copying the five valiant sons of the Living Spirit. In
between she set greed and lust, to copy the night watchmen, the Call and the
Answer. The fierce, poisonous fire of gluttony she let run free, to copy the
Srōšahrāy.

### 5. The cage of snakes

"When the five bodies of light have been held in such bitter bondage, they
forget their original mind, as though mad or drunk. It is as if someone
wove a cage out of poisonous snakes, all their heads turned inward,
spitting poison in every direction, and then took a man and hung him upside
down inside it. Pressed by the poison and hanging head down, his mind is
confused, and he has no leisure to think of his father and mother, his
family, or the joys he once had. The five light-natures in the body of
flesh, held prisoner by the Demon and suffering day and night, are just
the same.

### 6. The two ships and the Demon's counter-plot

"Then the Living Spirit made the two ships of light, to carry the good sons
across the sea of birth and death to their original realm, so that the
light-nature might come at last to peace and joy. When the Demon of
Enmity, the lord of greed, saw this, she became angry and jealous. She at
once made two forms, male and female, copying the two great ships of light,
the sun and moon, to lead the light-nature astray and make it board the
dark ships. These carry it into hell, to turn through the five paths of
rebirth, suffering every pain, hard to free at last.

### 7. The Light-Nous enters the old house

"But if an Envoy of Light appears in the world to teach beings and free
them from their sufferings, he first sends down the sound of the wondrous
Law through the gate of the ear. Then he enters the old house, holds the
great divine spell, binds the poisonous snakes and the evil beasts, and
does not let them be free. Then he brings the axe of wisdom, cuts down the
poisonous trees, digs out the stumps and the other weeds, and makes it all
clean. He adorns the palace, sets out the seat of the Law, and sits on it,
like a king who has defeated an enemy country and, inside it, adorns the
halls, sets up his jewelled throne, and judges all the people, good and
bad.

"The Light-Nous does the same. When he has entered the old city and broken
the enemy, he at once separates the two forces, light and darkness, so
that they are no longer mixed. First he subdues resentment and binds it in
the city of bone, so that the pure Air is freed from its bonds. Next he
subdues anger and binds it in the city of sinew, so that the wondrous Wind
is freed. Then he subdues lust and binds it in the city of the veins, so
that the Light is freed from its bonds. Then he subdues wrath and binds it
in the city of flesh, so that the wondrous Water is freed. Then he subdues
folly and binds it in the city of skin, so that the wondrous Fire is freed.
The two demons of greed and lust he binds in between, and the fierce fire
of hunger he lets run free.

"It is like a goldsmith refining gold: he must use fire, and without fire
the refining cannot be done. The Light-Nous is like the goldsmith, […] is
like the ore, and the Demon of Hunger is the fierce fire that refines the
fivefold body and makes it pure. In the bodies of the good, the great Envoy
Light-Nous uses the fire of hunger to great profit.

### 8. The old man, and the three days and two nights

"The five powers of light dwell in the mingled body. So the good man sifts
and chooses between the two forces and sets each apart. This body of
flesh is also called the Old Man. It is bone, sinew, veins, flesh and skin;
resentment, anger, lust, wrath and folly; and greed and gluttonous lust.
These thirteen together make up one body, and it copies the beginningless
realm of ignorance.

"The second dark night is the poisonous and evil thinking of the Demon of
Greed, all the natures that are not good: folly, lust, self-praise,
troubling others, anger, impurity, destruction, dissolution, death, deceit
and rebellion, the marks of darkness. Such is the fearful dark night of
ignorance. Its twelve dark hours are the sign of the first coming-forth of
the demons.

"For this reason the Light-Nous, the Great Wisdom, with good and skilful
means, sifts and saves the light-nature in this body of flesh and sets it
free. From his own five limbs he brings forth five gifts that strengthen the
light-nature. First, from the bright Sense he brings forth Compassion, and
adds it to the pure Air. Next, from the bright Mind [he brings forth
Faith, and adds it to the wondrous Wind. Next, from the bright Thought] he
brings forth Perfection, and adds it to the Light. Then from the bright Reflection he
brings forth Patience, and adds it to the pure Water. Then from the bright
Intention he brings forth Wisdom, and adds it to the pure Fire. Xrōštag and
Padvāxtag he adds, (with) wisdom, to the treasury of speech.

"Air, Wind, Light, Water and Fire; Compassion, Faith, Perfection, Patience
and Wisdom; and Xrōštag and Padvāxtag, together with the Light-Nous: these
thirteen copy the pure world of light. They are the sign of the Lord of
Light. One who keeps the precepts in full is like the day.

"The second day is Wisdom. Its twelve great kings, brought forth from the
Light-Nous, copy the fullness of the sun. They are the sign of Perfection.

"The third day is the seven kinds of *mahrāspand* (摩訶羅薩本). Each time
they enter the bodies of pure teachers and monks, they receive from the
Light-Nous the five gifts and the twelve hours, and become a complete day.
This copies the Srōšahrāy, the sign of great power.

"These three days and two nights are, both in the teachers and monks and in
the ordinary practitioners (行者), signs that there are two realms.

### 9. The five battles of the old man and the new

"At times the Old Man fights with the New Man of wisdom, just as at the
beginning the Demon of Greed planned to invade the realm of light. This is
the sign of it.

"From the dark, poisoned Sense of the Old Man, demons are brought forth,
and they fight against the Sense-body of the New Man. If the New Man does
not guard against the signs and forgets the bright Sense, then the signs
appear: in his conduct he has no compassion, and he feels resentment at
everything he meets. Then the light-nature is soiled, and both the pure
Sense-body and the stranger-nature lodging in it are harmed. But if he keeps
guard, heeds the signs and stays alert, if he drives resentment back and
practises compassion, then the Sense-body of the light-nature becomes pure
again, and the stranger-nature lodging there escapes every danger, leaps
for joy, gives thanks and goes its way.

"At times the New Man forgets to remember, and in the dark Mind demons are
brought forth that fight at once against the Mind of the New Man. Then there
are clear signs in that person's body: in his conduct he has no faith, and
he grows angry at everything he meets, and the stranger-nature lodging in
him is stained at once. But if the Mind-body of the light-nature remembers
again and does not forget its original mind, it wakes and drives the anger
off. Anger retreats and scatters, faith is as it was, and the
stranger-nature lodging there escapes its sufferings and reaches its
original realm.

"At times the New Man forgets to remember, and then from the dark, poisoned
Thought of ignorance demons are brought forth that fight at once against
the pure Thought-body of the New Man. Then there are clear signs in that
person: in his conduct he has no perfection, the heart of desire blazes,
and the stranger-nature lodging in him is stained at once. But if he
remembers and does not forget, if he guards the body of perfection well and
crushes every thought of desire so that it does not rise again, the
stranger-nature lodging there escapes all its sufferings, is made pure, and
reaches its original realm.

"At times demons are brought forth in the Reflection of ignorance that
fight at once against the Reflection of the New Man. If he forgets his
original Reflection, there will be signs: in his conduct he has no
patience, and he flies into a rage at everything he meets, and both natures,
the guest and the host, are stained. But if he remembers and does not
forget, if he wakes and resists the enemy, the raging heart withdraws, the
great power of patience comes back to support him, the stranger-nature
lodging there is gladly set free, the original nature is bright and clear,
and the Reflection-body is as it was.

"At times demons are brought forth in the Intention of ignorance that
fight at once against the Intention-body of the New Man. If he loses his
original Intention, there will be signs: in his conduct there is much
folly, and both natures, the guest and the host, are defiled. But if he
remembers and does not forget, then when folly rises he knows it at once,
quickly subdues it, strives with diligence, and gains wisdom. Through his
good deeds the stranger-nature lodging there is made pure, and the
Intention-body of the light-nature is clear and without stain.

"These are the five great battles. Between the New Man and the Old there is
from time to time a battle, and by these five powers the New Man guards
himself against the enemy, as the holy ones do in the great world, of which
these are the signs. Compassion copies the Envoy of Light who holds the
world (持世明使). Faith copies the Great King of the Ten Heavens. Perfection
copies the victorious Envoy who subdues the demons. Patience copies the
Envoy of Light who bears up the earth (地藏明使). Wisdom copies the Envoy of
Light who drives the light onward (催光明使).

"For this reason the holy ones of the past, and the teaching of the
present, say this: for one who has left home, the battle is not with
bounded bodies of flesh. It is with the unbounded, the poisonous natures of
the demons, fighting each other. So the pure
teachers who keep the precepts are of one kind with the holy ones. Why?
Because in subduing the demon enemies they are no different from the holy
ones.

### 10. The Light-Nous in the five cities of the New Man

"At times the troops of the Old Man fall back defeated, and the Light-Nous,
in the form of the Law, walks at ease through the countless lands of the
five worlds of the New Man. He enters the pure and wondrous city of Sense,
sets out the seat of the Law in its jewelled palace, and sits there at rest.
So too with the cities of Mind, Thought, Reflection and Intention: he enters
each of them in turn.

"If the Light-Nous is walking in the city of Sense, know that everything
this teacher preaches of the true Law is wondrous. He delights to speak of
the Great Light, the Three Constants and the Five Greats, of supernatural
transformations, and of all the perfect forms; and in the Law he speaks
above all of Compassion.

"If he is walking in the city of Mind, know that this teacher delights to
speak of the palaces of light of the sun and moon, their supernatural
transformations and their perfect power; and in the Law he speaks above all
of Faith.

"If he is walking in the city of Thought, know that this teacher delights
to speak of the great minister, the Srōšahrāy, and of the perfect silence
he brings forth by supernatural transformation; and in the Law he speaks above all of Perfection.

"If he is walking in the city of Reflection, know that this teacher
delights to speak of the five lights, and by supernatural power shows
[…]; and in the Law he speaks above all of Patience.

"If he is walking in the city of Intention, know that this teacher delights
to speak of the Envoys of Light of the past, the future and the present,
of their supernatural transformations and how they appear and vanish at
will; and in the Law he speaks above all of Wisdom.

"So the wise, looking closely at a teacher, know in which land the
Light-Nous is dwelling.

"If there are pure *dēnāvar* who hold to the supreme true Law in this way
and do not turn back until the end of their lives, then when their lives
end, the Old Man and his troops, the dark powers of ignorance, fall into
hell, with no time of coming out. But the Light-Nous at once leads his own
army of light, his pure household, straight to the realm of light, where
they are free of fear for ever and always enjoy happiness.

"The *Gospel* (應輪經) says: 'If the *dēnāvar* have the good Law in their
bodies, the Father and the Son of Light and the Pure Wind of the Law are
always present and moving in their bodies.' The Father of Light is the
supreme Lord of Light of the realm of light. The Son of Light is the light
of the sun and moon. The Pure Wind of the Law is the Light-Nous.

"The *Epistles* (寧萬經) say: 'If the *dēnāvar* have the good Law, then
pure light, great power and wisdom are all present in their bodies.' This
is the New Man, perfect in merit.

### 11. The Light-Nous as gardener: the dead trees and the living

"Listen closely, all of you. The great Envoy, the Light-Nous, enters this
world's upside-down [city], its crooked villages, its rotten old house, and
comes to the palace of the Demon. Because her (old city) was being
destroyed, the Demon of Greed built a new and filthy city, and in her folly
gave free rein to the five desires.

"At times the White Dove, the wondrous Pure Wind, the valiant son of the
Law, the son of the Great Holy One, enters this city. Looking about on all
four sides, he sees only smoke and fog, crooked walls all around, and
countless villages. Having looked, he walks on by stages until he reaches
the top of the city. Gazing straight down from there, he sees seven
jewelled pearls, each of them beyond price, and all of them wrapped and
covered in filth.

"Then the Light-Nous first takes rich, fertile, good ground and sows in it
the supreme seed of his own light. From his own body he brings out patterns
and treasures for his own gain, a great profit in trade. He adorns the inner
nature in every way and completes it, to be a supporting pillar. Leaning on
this pillar, the true seed can climb out of the five-layered dark pit of
ignorance, just as in the great realm the First Thought and the Living
Spirit each have five sons, who serve as pillars of support for the five
bodies of light.

"So the Light-Nous, the skilful farmer, levels and fills in the five rugged
grounds of evil ignorance. First he clears away the thorns and all the
poisonous weeds and burns them with fire. Next he cuts down the five kinds
of poisonous trees. When the five dark grounds have been levelled and
cleared, he sets up halls and palaces for the New Man, and plants every kind
of fragrant flower and jewelled tree in its gardens. After that he adorns
palaces, jewelled thrones, terraces and halls for himself, and next he builds
palaces too for the countless companions on his left and right. By his own
majestic power the Light-Nous builds and completes all these things. He also
turns over the dark ground of poison, evil, greed and lust, and turns it
upside down. Then the five pure bodies of the light-nature are gradually
able to stretch out and breathe. The five bodies are Sense, Mind, Thought,
Reflection and Intention. Then in that pure, five-layered jewelled ground the
Light-Nous plants five kinds of supreme jewelled trees, bright and
glorious, and on five jewelled terraces of light he lights five ever-burning
jewelled lamps of light.

"When the Light-Nous has given the five gifts, he first drives out the
dark Sense of ignorance and cuts down the five kinds of poisonous, evil dead
trees.

"(The first is the tree of the dark Sense.) Its root is resentment, its
trunk is obstinacy, its branches are anger, its leaves are hatred, its
fruit is division, its taste is indifference, and its colour is mockery
and dislike.

"Next he drives out the dark Mind of ignorance and cuts down its dead tree.
Its root is unbelief, its trunk is forgetfulness, its branches are flattery
and sloth, its leaves are obstinacy, its fruit is vexation, its taste is
greed, and its colour is refusal and concealment.

"Next he drives out the dark Thought of ignorance and cuts down its dead
tree. Its root is lust, its trunk is sloth, its branches are obstinacy, its
leaves are arrogance, its fruit is ridicule, its taste is craving, and its
colour is lustful love and every impure deed, done first and regretted
after.

"Next he drives out the dark Reflection and cuts down its dead tree. Its
root is wrath, its trunk is folly, its branches are unbelief, its leaves are
dullness, its fruit is contempt, its taste is pride, and its colour is
slighting others.

"Next he drives out the dark Intention and cuts down its dead tree. Its
root is folly, its trunk is heedlessness, its branches are sluggishness,
its leaves are gazing at one's own reflection and thinking oneself without
equal, its fruit is outdoing everyone in finery and dress, its taste is the
love of necklaces, pearls, rings, bracelets and every kind of jewel strung
about the body, and its colour is the craving for food of a hundred flavours
to fatten the body of flesh.

"Such trees are called dead trees. In this dark cave of ignorance the Demon
of Greed plants them diligently. Then the Light-Nous uses the sharp mattock
and axe of wisdom and cuts them down one after another. In the ground of the
original nature he plants his own five supreme, pure, jewelled trees of
light, waters them with the water of ambrosia, and they bear immortal
fruit.

"First he plants the tree of Sense. Its root is compassion, its trunk is
joy, its branches are gladness, its leaves are gentleness towards all, its
fruit is peace, its taste is reverent care, and its colour is
steadfastness.

"Next he plants the pure and wondrous jewelled tree of the Mind. Its root
is faith, its trunk is trust, its branches are awe, its leaves are
watchfulness, its fruit is diligent study, its taste is reading and
recitation, and its colour is peace and joy.

"Next he plants the tree of Thought. Its root is perfection, its trunk is
good will, its branches are dignified conduct, its leaves are truth that
adorns every deed, its fruit is true speech without any falsehood, its
taste is preaching the pure true Law, and its colour is delight in meeting
one another.

"Next he plants the tree of Reflection. Its root is patience, its trunk is
calm, its branches are endurance, its leaves are the precepts, its fruit is
fasting and hymns, its taste is diligent practice, and its colour is zeal.

"Next he plants the tree of Intention. Its root is wisdom, its trunk is
understanding the meaning of the Two Principles, its branches are eloquence
in making the Law clear, and its leaves are adaptability and knowing the
moment, able to overturn other teachings and to build up the true Law. Its
fruit is skill in question and answer, speaking well as the occasion
needs. Its taste is skill in parables that bring people to understanding.
Its colour is gentle and meek words, so that whatever is said pleases
everyone.

"Such trees are called living trees. Then the Light-Nous takes these sweet
trees and plants them in the five grounds of the self-nature, on all four
sides of the jewelled throne in the wondrous palace of that new city, and
in all its gardens and pavilions.

### 12. Compassion, the king of the virtues

"Among them the king is Compassion. Compassion is the ancestor of all
merits: like the bright sun, first among all lights; like the full moon,
most honoured among the stars; like a king's crown of flowers, first among
all ornaments; like the fruit, the best part of every tree; like the
light-nature in the dark body, wondrous beyond compare within it; like
plain salt, which gives flavour to every fine dish; like a king's seal,
which is obeyed wherever it is stamped; like the bright moon-pearl, first
among all jewels; like clear glue, which makes all the colours of a painting
fast; like lime, which makes white whatever it is spread on; like a palace
with a king inside it, which is made splendid and pure because of the king.
Compassion is the same. Where there is compassion, there is the good Law.
Without compassion, none of the merits one cultivates comes to anything.
That is why it is called king.

"Within Compassion there is also Faith. Faith is the mother of all good
things: like a queen, who helps the king to care for everyone; like the
power of fire, which ripens all things and brings out every flavour; like
the sun and moon, most honoured beyond compare among all forms, spreading
their light everywhere and nourishing everything. Compassion and Faith
bring all merits to perfection in the same way.

"Compassion and Faith are also the foundation of the light-causes of the
holy ones of the past and the future, and the wondrous gate to complete
insight. They are also the narrow path, wide enough only for the side of a
foot, along the great sea of the passions of the three worlds. Out of a
hundred thousand, hardly one can enter this path. But whoever enters it and
follows this way is born in the pure land, leaves suffering behind, is set
free, is without fear for ever, and has constant joy, peace and purity.

### 13. The days of light and the nights of darkness in the body

"Again, in the dark body of the Demon, the Light-Nous makes the great suns
of the light of wisdom shine through, and subdues the two kinds of dark
night of ignorance, copying the supreme signs of the light.

"The first day is the Light-Nous. Its twelve hours are the twelve great
kings of the victorious form. They copy the supreme signs of the pure
world of light.

"The second day is the pure seed of the New Man. Its twelve hours are the
twelve kings of light brought forth in turn. It is also the wondrous robe
of the victorious form of Jesus (夷數), which is given to the light-nature.
With this wondrous robe it adorns the inner nature and makes it perfect, so
that it is drawn up and rises and leaves the filthy land for ever. The day
of the New Man copies the vast Srōšahrāy. Its twelve hours copy the five
sons of light of the First Thought and the five of the Living Spirit,
together with Xrōštag and Padvāxtag. Together these make thirteen pure
bodies of light, and form one day.

"The third day is the voice of the Call and the Answer. Its twelve hours are
the wondrous Sense, Mind, Thought, Reflection and Intention, together with
Compassion, Faith, Perfection, Patience and Wisdom. This is the Call and
Answer.

"The fourth day copies the Envoy of Light of the sun in the great realm,
and Compassion and the rest. Its twelve hours copy the twelve maidens
brought forth in the palace of the sun. Their light is full and round, and
together they make one day.

"Then there are also two kinds of dark night. The first night is the Demon
of Greed. Its twelve hours are bone, sinew, veins, flesh and skin, and
resentment, hatred, anger, lust, wrath, folly, greed and the fire of
hunger: such impure poisons as these. They copy the first dark night of
the beginningless ignorance of the realm of darkness. The second night is
the blazing flame of fierce, poisonous desire. Its twelve hours are the
twelve kinds of dark, poisonous thinking. These dark nights copy the signs
of the first rising of the demons.

"Then the day of the Light-Nous faces the heavy, gloomy dark night of
ignorance, and by the power of light subdues the dark nature, so that all of
it falls back and scatters. For this reason it copies the sign of the first
Envoy of Light subduing the demons.

### 14. The twelve kings of light

"Again, in the body of ignorance, the Light-Nous subdues the demons in
every way, freely, like a king in his palace who rewards and punishes
without fear.

"The forms of the Light-Nous are these: first, the Great King; second,
Wisdom; third, Constant Victory; fourth, Joy; fifth, Diligence; sixth,
Equality; seventh, the Believing Heart; eighth, Patience; ninth,
Straightforwardness; tenth, Merit; eleventh, Concord of One Heart; twelfth,
Light Within and Without.

"When these twelve great hours of light enter the five lands of Sense,
Mind, Thought, Reflection and Intention, each one breeds countless lights,
and each brings forth fruit without measure too. The fruit appears in full
in the pure community. When a *dēnāvar* is complete in the twelve hours of
light, know that this teacher is different from the crowd. Being different
means this: the teachers and bishops always have kindness, goodness and
gentleness of their own in body and mind, and are calm and at one with
others. This is the sign of it. It is the first budding of the twelve trees
of form. On these trees supreme jewelled flowers always open, and when they
have opened their brightness shines everywhere. Between each flower and the
next, countless buddhas are brought forth, each giving birth to the next,
bringing forth countless bodies.

### 15. The twelve signs of the Elect

**(1) The Great King.** "If a *dēnāvar* holds within the tree of the first,
the Great King, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he does not
like to stay long in one place, just as a king is free and does not always
stay in one place: at times he goes out on progress with his troops, fully
armed and equipped, and makes all evil beasts and enemies go into hiding.
Second, he is not grasping: wherever he goes, if he receives offerings he
does not keep them secretly for his own use, but hands them all to the
community. Third, he is chaste and guards against faults. He can keep
himself pure, and he also urges others who study and practise to keep
themselves pure. Fourth, he always keeps close to his own honoured teacher
and to those who have wisdom. From the foolish, who love idle talk and
quarrels, he keeps well away. Fifth, he always loves to live together with
the pure community. Wherever he goes, he does not leave the others to sleep
alone in a room of his own. Anyone who does this is called a sick man. Just
as a sick man in the world, troubled by his illness, always likes to be
alone and does not want to be near his family and friends, so is one who
does not love the community.

**(2) Wisdom.** "If a *dēnāvar* who keeps the precepts holds within the
nature of wisdom, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he always
loves to praise pure and wise people, and loves to meet with the pure and
wise community; his heart is glad, and he never tires of them. Second, if his
own root of wisdom and understanding is narrow and weak, he feels no envy
when he hears the wise words of the wise. Third, in all the work he has to
do, he always studies diligently and his heart is never slack. Fourth, he
himself always studies diligently at wisdom, skilful means and all good
conduct, and urges others to practise them with him. Fifth, he keeps the
prohibitions and precepts with care and does not break them, and if he
breaks one by mistake, he at once confesses it openly before the community
and repents.

**(3) Constant Victory.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of
victory, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he does not like
slander, flattery or spite, and keeps away from people like that. Second,
he does not like quarrels and commotion. If there is a quarrel he quickly
withdraws, and if someone comes and forces a quarrel on him, he can bear
it patiently. Third, if someone gives way in debate, he does not press his
advantage or gloat over it. Fourth, he does not speak rashly, or speak
without being asked. If someone comes with a question, he thinks it over
before answering, so that in the end the questioner is not shamed by what
was said. Fifth, he goes along with what others say and does not contradict
them, nor does he insist on proving them wrong so as to show up their
fault. In the community of the Law his heart is in concord and causes no
division.

**(4) Joy.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of joy, know that
this teacher has five signs. First, in all the prohibitions, precepts and
rules of conduct of the holy teaching he rejoices, one by one, and keeps
them with all his strength until the end of his life, never letting his
heart give them up. Second, he gladly and reverently keeps what the holy
ones laid down, one change of clothes a year and one meal a day, and does
not find it hard. Nor does he falsely claim that the holy ones made this
rule only as a temporary measure, misquoting scriptures and treatises to
say that one may eat twice, or that those who seek liberation need not keep
this precept. Third, he studies only the pure Law of his own school, and
does not go looking for other, corrupt teachings. Fourth, his heart is
always humble, and he does not put himself above his fellow students.
Fifth, if he holds a lower rank, he does not overstep it; if he holds the
highest place as head, he regards the community as himself and loves them
all without favouring anyone.

**(5) Diligence.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of
diligence, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he does not like
sleep that gets in the way of the work of the Way. Second, he always loves
reading and recitation, and spurs his heart on without slackening. When
fellow students teach him, he is especially glad and grateful, and does not
resent being taught. He is always diligent himself, and urges others to be
so too. Third, he always loves to expound the pure, true Law. Fourth, in
hymns, prayers and recitation, in chanting, copying, remembering and
reflecting, he lets no time go by wasted. Fifth, he keeps the prohibitions
and precepts firmly, without a lapse.

**(6) Truthfulness.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of
truthfulness, know that this teacher has five signs. First, all the
scriptures and teachings he speaks are true. He keeps wholly to the holy
teaching and does not proclaim it falsely: where something is, he says it
is; where it is not, he says it is not. Second, his heart and intention are
always at one with the truth, and he does not wait for outside events to
set his rule by. Third, the precepts and practices he keeps are always
true, whether he is alone or with others; his heart is not divided. Fourth,
he is always settled in his heart towards his own teacher, serves him with
all his strength, and has no doubts, until the end of his life, with no
other intention. Fifth, he urges his fellow students to practise, and
guides everyone with truthful conduct.

**(7) The Believing Heart.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of
a believing heart, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he
believes the meaning of the Two Principles; his heart is pure and without
doubt; he leaves the darkness and follows the light, as the holy ones have
said. Second, his heart is settled in all the precepts and rules. Third, he
does not dare to add or take away a single sentence or a single word of the
holy scriptures. Fourth, he is glad of everything that benefits the true
Law and helps it with his heart. If he sees it harmed or troubled by the
demons, he feels compassion and shares in the grief. Fifth, he does not
falsely spread the faults of others, nor slander, carry tales or speak with
a forked tongue. His nature is always gentle, honest and undivided.

**(8) Patience.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of patience,
know that this teacher has five signs. First, his heart is always kind and
good, and no rage arises in it. Second, he is always glad and no anger
rises in him. Third, he bears no grudge anywhere. Fourth, his heart is not
hard and his mouth speaks no coarse or evil words. He always speaks gently,
in a way that pleases everyone. Fifth, whether inside (the community) or
outside it, if evils and troubles come to meet him and insult him, he can
bear them all, gladly and without resentment.

**(9) Straightforwardness.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of
straightforwardness, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he is not
bound by the passions; he is glad of himself, pure and straightforward.
Second, in the Law, whatever is asked of him, great or small, he receives
it with respect, and answers gladly and well. Third, with his fellow
students he does not argue back, or defend his own shortcomings and grow
angry. Fourth, his words and deeds match; his heart is always honest; he
does not go looking for others' faults and so start quarrels. Fifth, if
brothers within the Law have hearts that differ from the holy teaching, he
at once keeps away from them, does not live with them or keep close to
them, and does not join forces with them to trouble the good community.

**(10) Merit.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature of merit, know
that this teacher has five signs. First, nothing he says harms anyone. He
always uses a kind heart and skilful means, and can make everyone glad.
Second, his heart is always pure; he holds nothing against others, nor does
he do wrong so as to make others angry. His speech is always gentle and
free of the four faults (of speech). Third, he is not jealous of those above
him or those below. Fourth, he does not take scriptures, treatises or
disciples away from the community. Wherever he goes, he is glad to stay in
whatever pure dwelling there is, and does not choose one that is fine and
pleasant. Fifth, he always loves to teach and admonish all people, with
skill and wisdom, so that they practise the true Way.

**(11) Concord of One Heart.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the nature
of a heart in concord, know that this teacher has five signs. First, in the
wisdom, skilful means and conduct taught by the masters of the Law, the
teachers and bishops, he follows every point and does not dare to change
it, and does not insist on his own views. Second, he always loves concord
and living together with the community, and does not want to live apart,
each pursuing different plans. Third, his heart is in concord with the
others, and because of this concord the offerings received together become
merit for all. Fourth, he always has the respect, offerings, love and praise
of the hearers. Fifth, he always loves to keep far from frivolity,
joking and laughter, and from quarrels, and guards well what is within and
without, keeping the two natures in concord.

**(12) Light Within and Without.** "If a pure *dēnāvar* holds within the
nature of light in both, know that this teacher has five signs. First, he
pulls up the filthy heart and does not let it be greedy or lustful, so
that his light-nature is always free. Faced with women, he can see them as
unreal, and is not held back by any form, like a bird flying high that does
not fall into the net. Second, he does not form special, close friendships
with the hearers, nor cling to the hearers' houses as though they were his
own. If he sees losses or troubles in a lay household outside the Law, his
heart is not grieved; if they gain some profit or happy event, his heart is
unchanged too. Third, walking or standing, sitting or lying down, he does not
pamper the body of flesh by seeking fine soft clothes, bedding, food,
medicines, birds, horses or carriages to make it splendid. Fourth, he always
remembers the day of the end of life, with its dangers, pains and distress.
He always keeps impermanence and the King of Equal Justice before his eyes,
and never for a moment lets them go. Fifth, he himself is gentle and
yielding. He does not trouble his brothers or friends, or make them angry,
nor does he hope to prove anything against them and give others a bad name.
He can always keep his heart steady and abide in the pure Law.

### 16. The Envoy's charge

"Such as these are called the jewelled trees of the twelve kings of light.
For your sake I have brought them here from the world of light, where joy
is constant. I wish to plant these trees in your pure community. You men and
women of excellent form and good wisdom must each plant these trees in your
pure hearts and make them grow, like good ground free of sand and salt,
where one seed sown yields ten thousand, and so on, to a number without
measure. Now, if you want to win the pure fruit of the supreme great light,
you must all adorn yourselves with these jewelled trees and make them
complete. Why? Because, good sons, by the fruit of these trees you will
escape the four hardships and every kind of bodily existence, leave birth
and death behind, be victorious for ever, and come to the place of peace and
joy."

### 17. The hymn of the teachers

When the teachers (慕闍) in the assembly heard this scripture, they leapt for
joy and marvelled at what they had never heard before. The gods and good
spirits, bounded and unbounded, the kings, the
ministers, the men and women, and the four orders of the community,
countless beyond number, heard this scripture and were all filled with joy,
and every one of them was able to set his heart on the supreme Way, like
plants meeting the spring sun: all of them grew, flowered, bore fruit and
ripened, except for rotten roots, which could not grow.

Then the teachers bowed their heads to the Envoy of Light, knelt upright
with their hands joined, and said:

"You alone are the Great Holy One, alone honoured in the three worlds, the
compassionate father and mother of all beings, the great guide of the three
worlds, the great healing lord of all who have life. You are the wondrous
space that can hold all forms; the high heaven that embraces everything;
the true ground that bears true fruit; the great sea of ambrosia for all
beings; the vast fragrant mountain of all treasures; the diamond pillar that
bears up the multitude; the skilful, wise helmsman on the great sea; the
compassionate hand that rescues from the fiery pit; the giver of constant
life in the midst of death; the nature within the light-nature of beings;
the bright gate of release from all the strong prisons of the three
worlds."

The teachers spoke again to the Envoy of Light and said: "Only the One
Great Light can praise the holy virtue. With our tongues of flesh and our
poor wisdom we cannot know even a small part in ten million of the merit and
wisdom of the Tathāgata. Now we stir up our small virtue and small wisdom
and offer a little of our meaning in praise of the Holy One's great mercy.
We pray only that the Great Holy One will look on us with compassion, and
take away and destroy the heavy sins of ignorance that we have committed
through countless ages. We will not now dare to be careless; we will all
hold to the supreme jewelled trees and make them complete.

"By this water of the Law, wash away our dust and heavy stains, so that
our light-nature may always be pure. By this medicine of the Law and the
great divine spell, heal the heavy sicknesses we have had for many ages,
so that all of them are cured. By this firm armour of wisdom, clothe us, so
that we overcome our enemies. By these wondrous robes and crowns of every
form, adorn us, so that we are complete. By this pattern of light of the
original nature, seal us, so that we are not scattered and lost. By this
sweet food of a hundred flavours, satisfy us, so that we are free of hunger
and thirst. By this countless wondrous music, delight us, so that we are
free of sorrow and trouble. By these many rare treasures, provide for us,
so that we become rich. By this net of light, draw us out of the great sea
and set us in the jewelled ship.

"Now we are of excellent form and great blessing, for we have seen the
special marks and beauty of the Great Holy One, and have heard the wondrous
gate of the Law told above. It has swept away the stains of our passions,
our hearts have been opened and awakened, we have received the
wish-granting pearl, and in its majestic light we have been able to walk
the [high] way. The holy ones of the past, beyond counting, all passed
through this gate, escaped the four hardships and every bodily existence,
reached the world of light, and receive joy without measure. We pray only
that every light-nature in the future that meets this gate of light,
whether it sees or hears it, may, like the holy ones of old and like us
today, hear the Law with joy, have its heart opened and awakened, receive it
with reverence on bowed head, and have no doubts."

Then the whole great assembly, having heard this scripture, believed and
accepted it according to the Law, and joyfully put it into practice.

## Notes to this edition

- **The base text** is the Taishō edition (vol. 54, no. 2141B, pp. 1281a–1286a)
  as transcribed by CBETA, checked against the logic of the passage.
  Chavannes and Pelliot's French (1911, public domain) will be used to check
  the draft. The translation does not reproduce CBETA's notes or
  punctuation.
- **路㑥 and 業羅決** are the male and female demons who make Adam and Eve:
  Ašaqlūn and Nebrōēl in the Western sources. The Chinese renderings are
  transcriptions, given here in pinyin.
- **[…] in §7**: the characters 㘈𠺕而云䁥 transcribe a foreign word.
  Chavannes and Pelliot read it *yi-lieou-eul-yun-ni* and leave it
  untranslated. In the comparison it stands for the gold ore, the mixture
  being refined.
- **放** is often used for 仿, "to imitate", as Chavannes and Pelliot saw
  (§§3, 4, 6). In §4 this settles the sense: the five passions copy the five
  sons of the Living Spirit.
- **有礙無礙**, "the bounded and the unbounded" (§§3, 9, 17), follows
  Chavannes and Pelliot ("le limité et l'illimité"). It points to the
  Manichaean contrast between finite form and unbounded elements.
- **客性**, "stranger-nature": the light that is a guest in the body.
- **§8, the five gifts**: the manuscript has dropped a clause, so that the
  Mind is paired with Perfection and the Light. The bracketed words restore
  it, following Chavannes and Pelliot (1911, p. 541 n.): Faith comes from
  the Mind and is added to the Wind, and Perfection from Thought. The list
  of thirteen that follows has all five virtues.
- **具足, "Perfection"**: literally "completeness, sufficiency". Chavannes
  and Pelliot render it *contentement*, "contentment".
- **摩訶羅薩本** (§8) transcribes Middle Persian *mahrāspand*, the "holy
  words" or elements.
- **The five Envoys in §9** are the five sons of the Living Spirit: the
  Keeper of Splendour (Splenditenens), the King of Honour, Adamas, the
  Bearer (Omophoros, "earth-store"), and probably the King of Glory, who
  turns the wheels that raise the light.
- **§13, "the great suns"**: the text says 三大光明惠日, "three great suns of
  light", but goes on to list four days. The number is left out of the
  translation.
- **§17, "the [high] way"**: the manuscript has 缶道, "jar way", which is
  probably a copying error. "High" is a guess and is marked as one.
- **平等王** (§15.12), "the King of Equal Justice", is the judge of the
  dead.
- **§14, the sixth king** is 平等, "Equality", but in §15 the sixth sign is
  headed 真實, "Truthfulness". Both readings are kept.
- **應輪經 and 寧萬經** (§10) are probably the Gospel and the Epistles. The
  Chinese *Compendium* lists Mani's books as 大應輪部 (the Gospel) and 泥萬部
  (the Epistles, Middle Persian *nāmag*), and 寧萬 looks like another
  transcription of the same word. Chavannes and Pelliot, writing before the
  *Compendium* was identified, took the titles literally: "Book of the Wheel
  of Retributions" and "Book of the Universe".
- **§10, "the great minister"** (大相): Chavannes and Pelliot point out that
  the word order rules out "the great form of the Srōšahrāy", so 相 here
  means "minister".
- **§10, […]**: four characters are missing from the manuscript in the
  passage on the city of Reflection (Chavannes and Pelliot).
