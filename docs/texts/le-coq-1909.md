## Introduction

In 1909 Le Coq published two short sets of Turfan texts.

The first is a leaf (T II D 178e) from the same Manichaean book as other
Old Turkish leaves from Qočo. It tells the episode of the Buddha legend in
which the Bodhisattva prince, riding out with his groom Chandaka, sees an
old man for the first time. Le Coq argued that the book was Manichaean,
not Buddhist: it is bound in the western way, uses the Manichaean
punctuation, and its other leaves, by the same scribes, speak of the
Messiah, the Manichaean Gospel and Iranian gods. He suggested that the
Manichaeans, not the Christians, first brought the story of the Bodhisattva
prince to the West, where it became the legend of *Barlaam and Josaphat*.

The second set is written in Turkic runic script on paper: a Middle Persian
hymn with Turkish rubrics, some Middle Persian scraps, and a double leaf in
Turkish (T M 342) from a story.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German. Gaps are
marked [ … ]; words Le Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in
parentheses are added for sense.

Chandaka answered him with reverence: "O prince, this man was once a

Then the Bodhisattva said: "[ … ] we too, living long, [ … ] so [ … ] there

and his mother the queen, having heard (this), both came; whatever they
asked their son, he gave no answer. Then Śuddhodana the king said to all
his officials and nobles, sternly: "[ … ]," he said. "Pursue (?) [ … ]
going; every [ … ], every people [ … ] the people [ … ]" *(two lines

## Notes to this edition

- **The Bodhisattva legend.** The groom Chandaka and King Śuddhodana are
  the figures of the Buddha's own story; in *Barlaam and Josaphat* the
  prince likewise meets an old man and a sick man and returns home troubled.
  Le Coq quotes an earlier discussion of the title *Būdhāsaf* in al-Nadīm's
  book list, which is generally explained as "Bodhisattva" and lies behind
  the name *Josaphat*.
- **The Christian fragment** published with it (T II B 1, from Bulayïq) is
  an apocryphal Christian text in Turkish and is not Manichaean, so it is
  not given here.
- **Runic script.** These are among the few texts in Turkic runes written on
  paper; Le Coq published them in part for the new letter forms they show.
