## Introduction

Le Coq's no. 9 is a set of damaged double leaves (T II D 78a–k) from an
Uyghur book of European form, written with a careless brush on coarse brown
paper, found in ruin K at Qočo. They hold a long hymn of prayer, spoken by a
penitent to "my God", who is the Saviour, the master-physician, the father
who lights up the mind. Le Coq called it "the great hymn fragment, in which
Mani is called on as *new day, new month, new year*". Only the first five
pieces (i–v) are well enough preserved to translate; for the rest (vi–xi)
Le Coq printed the Turkish words that survive, mostly without German.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German, which he
called "a first attempt at interpretation". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words
Le Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in parentheses are added
for sense.

## Notes to this edition

- **"Our new day, our new month"** (v): the same images as in the
  Middle Persian hymns of the new moon and the full moon to Jesus and Mani
  ([M 176](turfan-fragments-2.html)) and Le Coq's description of this hymn,
  "new day, new month, new year".
- **Sumnu** (i, ii) is a demon's name that Le Coq left unexplained; "the
  grey-haired demoness of death" is the hag of the hymns on the soul after
  death ([Old Turkish hymns](turkish-manichaica-2.html)).
- **"The master-physician"**: the same title is given to Jesus and to Mani
  in the Chinese Hymnscroll ("King of Physicians").
