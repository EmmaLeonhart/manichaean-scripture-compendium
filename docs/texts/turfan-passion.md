## Introduction

The Manichaeans read the Christian Gospels and retold them in their own
books. Two leaves from Turfan quote the end of the Gospel story in Middle
Persian: Pilate's hand-washing and the soldiers' orders (M 18), the trial
before Pilate and Herod and the mocking (M 132), and the women at the tomb
(M 18). The text weaves the four Gospels together. Müller noticed that it
follows the Syriac Gospels rather than the Greek: it calls Salome
*Šalōm* and the governor *hegmōn*, as the Syriac does. One leaf of M 132 is
in Turkish; Müller printed only the Middle Persian leaf.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's first
edition of 1904, the only public-domain edition, which he called "a first
attempt". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense. Gospel parallels are given
in the notes.

## Notes to this edition

- **Gospel parallels.** Pilate's words and the guard: Matthew 27:24 and
  27:62–66. The women and the spices: Mark 16:1 and Luke 24:1. "Do not seek
  the living among the dead … remember how he told you in Galilee": Luke
  24:5–7. "He goes ahead of you to Galilee … tell his disciples and Peter":
  Mark 16:7. "My kingdom is not of this world": John 18:36. Herod: Luke
  23:7. The mocking: Matthew 27:28–30 and Mark 15:17–19.
- **"Šalōm" and "hegmōn"**: Müller points out that the Syriac Peshitta has
  *Šalōm* at Mark 16:1, and *hegmōn* for "governor" at Matthew 27:2. So the
  Manichaean text was made from the Syriac Gospels.
- **"Arsenia (?)"** (M 18): a third woman named alongside Mary and Šalōm,
  not in the canonical Gospels. Müller suggested Arsenia or Arsinoe.
- **"At the first cock-crow"**: the Gospels say "very early" or "at
  dawn"; the cock-crow is the Manichaean text's own wording.
