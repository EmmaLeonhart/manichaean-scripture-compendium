## Introduction

More small hymns and prayers from Müller's 1904 edition. Several call on
Mani as the life-giver who "brings the dead to life and gives light to
those in darkness", and ask to be saved "from this being-born-and-dying".
Two (M 551 and M 789) promise "what you have not seen with the eye, nor
heard with the ear, nor grasped with the hand", a saying also known from
Paul (1 Corinthians 2:9) and the Gospel of Thomas (17). M 173 is an
alphabetic hymn that begins "Ālaf, the first, are you, Lord, and Tau, the
last". M 74 and M 543 are prayers for a lay patron and for a ruler or
church leader of Khorasan.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's first
edition of 1904, the only public-domain edition, which he called "a first
attempt". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense.

## The translation

### M 102: a hymn to "our lords of praised name"

*A leaf fragment, large octavo. Blue heading: "Our lords of praised
name".*

[ … ] of the religion, because he is a great ruler [ … ] into, and he is
full of splendour. He is the one over whom an enemy [has no power],
because he is a friend [ … ]. He is the one over whom no foul smell [ … ]
has come, because he is all fragrance. He is the one whom death does not
[touch], because he is all life. He is the one over whom darkness [has no
power], because he is strong light [ … ] full. He is the one who [ … ],
because he is all living and light [ … ] darkness not [ … ] welfare and
joy [ … ]

**Back.** *Blue heading, untranslated by Müller: "Mari zindag Yišō' ispasag" (perhaps "Lord, living Jesus, …").* (Müller gave
no German for this side. It is a hymn of praise with the words "great,
living ruler", "pure, shining", "praised", "the blessed kingdoms" and "the
angels".)

### M 311: a prayer to Mani

*A fragment of a small leaf. Line 5, a red heading in the Parthian
"dialect", untranslated.*

Mani, son of the gods, lord, life-giver (?) of the faith, great chosen one:
I bring you worship. May you be of shining face, Mani, life-giving lord!
He brings the dead to life and gives light to those in darkness. Lead (?)
me, Mani, lord; and Maiden of Light, answer me with splendour [ … ]

**Back, lower half, line 10.** O shining Mani, lord of growing fame,
life-giver! Protect me in the body. Jesus, Lord, save my soul from this
being-born-and-dying! Save my soul from this being-born-and-dying! Splendid
is your shining throne [ … ]

### M 551: a prayer

[ … ]

**Back.** *A prayer.*

[ … ] may you live always joyfully, for long! May the angels protect you,

### M 38: a prayer to Mani and the Maiden of Light

*A badly preserved leaf fragment; the front is rubbed away. A prayer. Back:*

Great Mithras, [ … ] messenger of the gods, interpreter of the chosen
religion. Jesus, Maiden of Light! Mār Mānī, Jesus, Maiden of Light! Mār
Mānī, make peace in me! O bringer of light, may you save my soul from this

### M 173: an alphabetic hymn

*A badly preserved upper half of a large leaf, with verses in double lines
in alphabetical order. These verses are well preserved:*

Ālaf, the first, are you, Lord, and Tau, the last, are joined (?) in you.
And your will and good deed have become perfect. All the gods and the
rulers, the yazads, the shining ones and the pure ones give praise with
many a "Holy!"

### M 543: a prayer for a leader

*A leaf fragment. A prayer.*

[ … ] and leader of the Mazdayasnian (?) faith. You, new teacher (?) of
Khorasan, and helper of those who have the good faith; for you were born
under a shining star in the line of rulers. Chosen are [ … ] Jesus the
Maiden, and Wahman [ … ] to this [ … ] angels [ … ] god (the rest is
destroyed).

**Back.** [ … ] good [ … ] May the rulers make supplication before you,
receive your command, and hear your teaching; may all bring you
satisfaction (?) by bowing and by entreaty before you. May the angels give
you victory over all your fellows; may the spirits [ … ] you, the glories
[ … ] you [ … ] (the rest is destroyed).

## Notes to this edition

- **"What you have not seen with the eye …"** (M 551, M 789): compare
  1 Corinthians 2:9 and the Gospel of Thomas, saying 17, which also has
  "what no hand has touched".
- **"Being-born-and-dying"** (M 311, M 38; Müller: *Geboren-Tot(sein)*,
  from *zādmurd*) is the Manichaean term for bodily existence, the round of
  rebirth.
- **"Ālaf … Tau"** (M 173): compare Revelation 22:13, "I am the Alpha and
  the Omega, the first and the last", here with the Syriac letters.
- **"The Mazdayasnian faith"** (M 543): Müller's reading *dēn māzdēs*; it
  is unusual in a Manichaean prayer and may be a title borrowed from
  Zoroastrian usage.
- **Mithras** (M 38): Mihr, the god of the sun, is in these texts a name of
  the Third Envoy or the Living Spirit.
