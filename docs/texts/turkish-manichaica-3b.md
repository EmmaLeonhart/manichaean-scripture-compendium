## Introduction

These are Le Coq's nos. 10 to 14 in his 1922 volume: two short hymns, a
leaf on alms and false Elect from the same book as the hearers' confession
(the [Xwāstwānīft](xwastwanift.html)), several leaves of a book of parables
(*brama*), and the start of his group of colophons and miscellaneous pieces,
which includes a scrap of the fables of Aesop in Turkish.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German, which he
called "first attempts at interpretation". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Le
Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in parentheses are added for
sense.

## Notes to this edition

- ***Śrī*** and ***baš*** (no. 11): Le Coq thought *baš* a form of the word
  for "hymn", and left open whether *śrī* invokes the Indian goddess of
  fortune.
- ***baqïr*** (no. 13) is a small weight; Le Coq gives it as about a tenth
  of an ounce.
- **Tušist** (no. 13) may be Tušita, the Buddhist heaven, here taken as the
  name of a god.
- **Aesop** (no. 14): Le Coq identified *Yosipas* with Aesop through the
  Syriac and Arabic fables of "Sophos", and compared a fable in Landsberger's
  edition, *Die Fabeln des Sophos* (1859), no. 66.
