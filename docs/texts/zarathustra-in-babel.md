## Introduction

This half leaf, found by Le Coq at Qočo in 1905, was the first Manichaean
work known to be written in the Uyghur script rather than the Manichaean
one. It tells of battles that a being called *Zrušč Burxan* fights against
demons and sorcerers in Babel, the holy city of the Manichaeans. Le Coq
identified *Zrušč Burxan* with Zarathustra, the prophet of the Iranians,
whom the Manichaeans counted among the Envoys of Light before Mani. He
dated the leaf to the Tang period (about 600–900).

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German. Gaps are
marked [ … ]; words Le Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in
parentheses are added for sense.

## The translation

**Front.** [ … ] (his) wise Law [ … ] the strong apostles (angels), who to
him [ … ] (?) [ … ] saying. The great one among those demons fled and
appeared (?) [ … ] was a tree (?). The great one among those demons, at
that tree (?) [ … ] seized the apostles, dragged them off and had them
slaughtered; the leaf of that tree (?) fell to the earth.

At that time the people who were in the city of Babel became hostile;
taking stones for the sorcerer [ … ] turned back towards them, their heads
[ … ] spoke so: "You [ … ]"

**Back.** He sat down on the top of that tree (?); in his heart he thought
so: "I will throw myself down, I will fall, I will kill Zrušč Burxan,"
so he thought.

At that time the sorcerers in the city of Babel took bow and arrow; [the
arrow], glancing back (?), struck his own vein of life. In this way the
demon died. The great one among the sorcerers was filled with shame.

Zrušč [Burxan] rose up from the place where he had been, and went to the
court of the city of Babel; so [ … ] (the rest is lost).

## Notes to this edition

- ***Zrušč Burxan***: *burxan* is the Turkish word for a buddha or prophet,
  used in Manichaean texts for the Envoys of Light. Le Coq took *zrušč* to
  be a form of Zarathustra's name. Mani taught that Zarathustra in Persia,
  the Buddha in India and Jesus in the West had come before him.
- **Babel** is called the holy city of the Manichaeans: Mani was born in
  Babylonia, and the head of the church lived there.
- Le Coq added a list of Old Turkish words from several Manichaean
  manuscripts, for the study of the language; it is not reproduced here.
