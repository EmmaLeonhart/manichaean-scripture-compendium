## Introduction

More of Le Coq's colophons and miscellaneous pieces (1922): rules of
conduct and fast days, a leaf on the commandments Mani gave his Elect, a
leaf in which the disciples are told to eat "the flesh of the innocent,
sinless lamb" without breaking its bones, prayers for a ruler (*Tängrikän*)
of Qočo and its twenty-two cities, and a prayer to Mani "who, when we came
to the Uyghur land, [gave] us strength and fortune".

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Le Coq's German, which he
called "first attempts at interpretation". Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Le
Coq marked as uncertain are marked (?); words in parentheses are added for
sense. The pieces are given in the order in which Le Coq printed them.

## Notes to this edition

- **The day of Ohrmizd** (the rules): the first day of the Iranian month,
  named after Ohrmizd; the great and small fast-days are the Manichaean
  fasts. Le Coq's reading of the word for "Ohrmizd" (*Vurmazt*) is a little
  uncertain.
- **"The five commandments"**: the commandments of the Elect. The ban on
  "dried blood" agrees with the Elect's rule against eating flesh and
  blood.
- **"The flesh of the innocent, sinless lamb … do not break its bones"**:
  compare Exodus 12:46 and John 19:36, said of the Passover lamb and of
  Jesus. The speaker, who "made appear twelve kinds of signs" and
  established "the Law of the Magi", is not named in what survives.
- **Qočo and the twenty-two cities**: the Uyghur kingdom of Qočo
  (the West Uyghur kingdom) and its towns; the *Tängrikän* is its ruler.
- **The Toquz Oγuz** ("Nine Oγuz") are the Uyghur tribal confederation.
