## Introduction

Theodore bar Konai, a teacher of the Church of the East, finished his
*Book of Scholia* in 791. Its eleventh book is a catalogue of heresies, and
its chapter on the Manichaeans gives a hostile life of Mani and then "a
sample of the absurd teaching of the impious Manes". The sample is a string
of sentences taken from Manichaean writings, perhaps from Mani's own books,
introduced again and again with "he says" and "Mani says". It is the
fullest and most exact account of the Manichaean myth in any language
outside the Manichaean texts themselves, and it keeps the Syriac names of
the gods.

Henri Pognon published the Syriac with a French translation in 1898, in an
appendix to his edition of Mandaean bowls. He noted that the Manichaean
passage uses Mandaean words and grammar, and suggested that Mani's books
were written in an Aramaic close to Mandaic.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Pognon's French of 1898.
Pognon's notes on the Syriac and Mandaic words are not reproduced; where he
marked a word as uncertain, it is marked (?). The copyists left gaps, which
are marked [ … ].

## The translation

### On Mani

Many stories have been told about the impious Manes. Some have said that
he was called Kourkabios, and that he had first studied the doctrines of
the sect of the Pure, because they had bought him; that the village where he
was born was called Abroumia; that his father was called Patiq; and that
the Pure, those who are called the white-robed, being unable to bear him,
expelled him from their sect and called him "vessel of evil", which is why
he was called Manes.

Others say that he was the freedman of the wife of Bados. This Bados had
been the disciple of a certain Skountianos, who had taken up the teachings
of certain Egyptian philosophers; for he had gone to Egypt and, for that
reason, had kept company with the wise men who lived in that country at the
time, and had studied the Egyptian and Greek literatures (?), as well as the
books of Pythagoras and of Proclus (?). He is said to have brought pagan
teachings into Christianity and taught the doctrine of the two principles,
one good and the other evil, like Proclus; he gave victory to the evil
principle, and to the good desire and love. This Skountianos had as his
disciple the Bados we have spoken of; the latter, who was earlier called
Tribinthos, composed, following the doctrines he had from Skountianos, four
books, which he entitled: the first the *Book of Mysteries*, the second the
*Gospels*, the third the *Book of the Treasure*, and the fourth the *Book of
Chapters*. After writing these works he went to Babylon, where he led many
people astray. While he was performing secret magical practices, he was
struck by the spirit and died. The woman who lived with him buried him. She
took everything Bados had left, and bought a little slave about seven years
old, named Kourkabios. After freeing him, she had him taught to read and
write, and to study the books of Bados. When his mistress had died and he
had become a man, he went into the countries where Bados had taught his
doctrines, changed his name there to Manes, claimed that the four books of
Bados were his own, and took his doctrines for himself.

He used the art of medicine together with magic, and though his opinions
on everything were pagan, he wished to use the name of the Messiah so that,
by this means, he could lead many people astray. He taught people to honour
the demons as gods and to worship the sun, the moon and the stars; he also
accepted omens and the horoscope, and rejected the law of Moses, the
prophets and the God who gave the law. Of our Saviour he said that he was
born and suffered in appearance, but was not really a man as he had seemed
to be. He says that bodies come from the evil principle, denies the
resurrection, claims that one part of the world comes from God and the other
from matter, and forbids eating what has had life. All who belong to his
religion are wicked: they slaughter men in devilish mysteries, prostitute
themselves without shame, have no pity and are without hope. As for Manes,
King Sapor flayed him, filled his skin with straw, and fixed it before the
gate of Beth-Lapat, a city of the Elamites.

### On his impure teaching

We must give in this book a sample of the absurd teaching and the
blasphemous opinions of the impious Manes, to the greater shame of the
Manichaeans.

He says that before the heaven and the earth and everything in them
existed, there were two principles, the one good, the other evil. The good
one dwelt in the land of light, and he calls him the Father of Greatness.
The Manichaeans say that outside him dwell his five dwellings: intelligence,
knowledge, thought, reflection and feeling. He calls the evil principle the
King of Darkness, and says that he dwells in his dark land, in his five
worlds: the world of smoke, the world of fire, the world of wind, the world
of the waters and the world of darkness.

He adds that when the King of Darkness planned to go up to the land of
light, these five dwellings were afraid. He adds that then the Father of
Greatness reflected and said: "I will send to the war none of these five
dwellings, which are my worlds, because they were created by me for
tranquillity and peace; but I will go myself and fight against him." He
says that the Father of Greatness called forth the Mother of Life, that the
Mother of Life called forth the First Man, and that the First Man called
forth his five sons, as a man puts on his armour for battle.

He says further that an angel named Nahašbaṭ went out before him, holding in
his hand the crown of victory; that the First Man spread the light before
him; and that, on seeing him, the King of Darkness reflected and said: "What
I sought far off, I have found close at hand." Then the First Man gave
himself, with his five sons, as food to the five sons of darkness, as a man
who has an enemy mixes a deadly poison in a cake and gives it to him. He
adds that when the sons of darkness had eaten them, understanding was taken
from the five shining gods, and through the poison of the sons of darkness
they became like a man bitten by a mad dog or a serpent.

He adds that the First Man came to himself again and prayed seven times to
the Father of Greatness. He called forth, as the second creation, the Friend
of the Lights; the Friend of the Lights called forth the Great Ban (the
Builder); the Great Ban called forth the Living Spirit; and the Living Spirit
called forth his five sons: the Ornament of Splendour from his intelligence,
the Great King of Honour from his knowledge, Adamas-Light from his reason,
the King of Glory from his thought, and the Bearer from his reflection.

They went to the land of darkness and found the First Man swallowed up by
the darkness, he and his five sons. Then the Living Spirit spoke a word,
and the word of the Living Spirit took the form of a sharp sword. He made the
image of the First Man appear and said to him: "Greetings to you, good one
among the wicked, shining one in the midst of darkness, god who dwells among
the beasts of wrath who do not know their honour!" The First Man answered
him: "Come in the peace of the dead (?); come, treasure (?) of calm and
peace!" He said further: "How are our fathers, the sons of light, in their
city?" The Caller answered him: "They are well." The Living Spirit, the
Caller and the Answerer came together and went up to the Mother of Life and
the Living Spirit. The Living Spirit put on the Caller, and the Mother of
Life put on the Answerer, her beloved son. They went down to the land of
darkness, to the place where the First Man and his sons were.

Then the Spirit commanded three of his sons that one should kill and
another flay the archons, the sons of darkness, and that they should bring
them to the Mother of Life. The Mother of Life stretched out the heaven from
their skins; she made eleven heavens, and they threw their bodies down on the
land of darkness. They made eight earths, and the five sons of the Living
Spirit each received a task (?). It was the Ornament of Splendour who held
the five shining gods by their loins, and below their loins the heavens
were spread out. It was the Bearer who, kneeling on one knee, bore the
earths. When the heavens and the earths had been made, the Great King of
Honour sat in the midst of the heaven and kept watch to guard them all.

Then the Living Spirit revealed his forms to the sons of darkness; he
purified some of the light they had swallowed, taking it from those five
shining gods, and made the sun, the moon, and more than a thousand lights.
She made the vessels: wind, water and fire. He went down and formed them
below, near the Bearer. The King of Glory called forth and set over them
wheels (?), so that they might rise over those archons cast down on the
earths, to serve the five shining gods, lest they be burned by the poison of
the archons [ … ].

Mani says that the Mother of Life, the First Man and the Living Spirit
began to pray and beseech the Father of Greatness. The Father of Greatness
heard them and called forth, as the third creation, the Messenger. The
Messenger called forth the twelve virgins with their robes, their crowns and
their attributes: the first was Royalty, the second Wisdom, the third
Victory, the fourth Persuasion, the fifth Purity, the sixth Truth, the
seventh Faith, the eighth Patience, the ninth Equity, the tenth Goodness, the
eleventh Uprightness, the twelfth Light. When the Messenger came to these
ships [ … ], he charged three servants to make them move; he charged the
Great Ban to build a new earth, and the three vessels (wheels?) to rise.

When the ships moved and reached the middle of the heaven, the Messenger
showed his forms, both the male form and the female form, and he was seen
by all the archons, the sons of darkness, male and female. At the sight of
the Messenger, who was beautiful in his forms, all the archons were filled
with desire: the males desired the female form and the females the male
form, and in their desire they began to give up the light they had
swallowed, taking it from the five shining gods. Then the sin that had been
imprisoned (?) in them mixed with the light that came out of the archons,
in the proportion in which it had been swallowed. They wanted to enter in,
but the Messenger hid his forms, and cut off the light of the five shining
gods, and the sin that was with them.

The sin that came out of the archons fell back on them, but they did not
take it back, like a man who loathes his own spittle. Then this sin fell
on the earth, half on the wet part and half on the dry. (On the wet part)
it turned into a horrible beast, like the King of Darkness. Adamas-Light
was sent against it, gave it battle and overcame it. He threw it on its
back, struck it in the aorta of its heart, thrust his shield over its
mouth, and set one foot on its thighs and the other on its breast. The sin
that had fallen on the dry part began to sprout in the form of five trees.

Mani says that those daughters of darkness were pregnant before, of their
own nature. Because of the beauty of the Messenger's forms, which they had
seen, they miscarried; their unborn young fell on the earth and ate the
buds of the trees. The abortions took counsel among themselves and
remembered the form of the Messenger they had seen. They said: "Where is the
form we saw?" Ašaqlūn, son of the King of Darkness, said to the abortions:
"Give me your sons and your daughters, and I will make you a form like the
one you saw." They brought them and gave them to him; but he ate the males
and gave the females to Namraēl, his companion. Namraēl and Ašaqlūn came
together; Namraēl conceived and bore to Ašaqlūn a son, whom she named Adam;
she conceived and bore a daughter, whom she named Eve.

Mani says further that Jesus the Splendour drew near to the senseless Adam
and woke him from a sleep of death, so that he might be freed from many
spirits (?). As a righteous man who finds a man possessed by a fearsome
demon and calms him by his art, so it was with Adam, since that friend
found him sunk in a deep sleep, woke him, made him move, drew him out of
sleep, drove the seducing demon from him, and bound far from him the great
troop of the archons.

Then Adam examined himself and knew who he was. Jesus showed him the
Fathers who dwell on high, and his own self, exposed to everything, to the
teeth of the panther, to the teeth of the elephant, swallowed by the
voracious, gulped down by those who gulp (?), eaten by the dogs, mixed and
imprisoned in all that exists, bound in the stench of darkness. Mani adds
that he made him stand up and taste of the Tree of Life. Then Adam looked
and wept. He raised his voice loud like a roaring lion, tore his hair, cried
out (?), beat his breast and said: "Woe, woe to the maker of my body, to the
one who bound my soul, and to the rebels who have enslaved me!"

## Notes to this edition

- **The life of Mani** in Theodore's first section is the hostile Christian
  legend first told in the *Acta Archelai* (the slave Kourkabios inheriting
  the books of Terebinthus-"Bados" and Scythianus); it is given here because
  it is part of the same chapter, but it is not Manichaean.
- **The gods** have their Syriac names: the Father of Greatness, the Mother
  of Life, the First Man; the Friend of the Lights, the Great Ban (the
  Builder), the Living Spirit; the Messenger (the Third Envoy). The five
  sons of the Living Spirit are the Ornament of Splendour (Splenditenens),
  the Great King of Honour, Adamas-Light, the King of Glory and the Bearer
  (Omophoros). Compare the same gods in Middle Persian and Turkish in [the
  Šābuhragān](shabuhragan.html), [the building of the
  world](turfan-cosmogony.html) and [Old Turkish texts from
  Qočo](turkish-manichaica-1.html).
- **The Caller and the Answerer** (Xrōštag and Padvāxtag in the Iranian
  texts) appear in the Chinese [Traité](traite.html) and Hymnscroll too.
- **"Called forth"**: Pognon translates the Syriac verb as "created" and
  notes that it has this sense in Mandaic; in the Manichaean myth the gods
  are not made but emanate, and "called forth" is used here.
- **Gaps.** Pognon noted that the copyists left out at least one passage
  before "these ships" (the making of the sun and moon as ships) and that a
  passage on the vessels is confused.
