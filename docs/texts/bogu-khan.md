## Introduction

Two short pieces published in 1912 in honour of Vilhelm Thomsen, who
deciphered the Old Turkic inscriptions.

**Le Coq's book fragment** is from a small Manichaean book in Uyghur script
found at Qočo in 1905. It is badly damaged, but enough names survive to show
that it was a work of history, one of the very few known from the
Manichaeans of Turkestan. It names the Uyghur khan Bögü coming to Qočo and
holding counsel "with the *možak*" about the settling of three *mahistaks*
"in the year of the Sheep", and it quotes "the wise Tonyuquq", the famous
Turkic statesman. Le Coq identified the khan with Bögü Khan (reigned
759–780), who made Manichaeism the religion of the Uyghur state, and
suggested that the year of the Sheep is 767.

**Müller's piece** (T II D 135) is the back of a leaf from the opening of a
Manichaean book in Middle Persian; on the front was a miniature of armed
warriors around a larger figure. The text blesses the "great ruler", a khan
whose Turkish titles end "the emanation of Mani", and his court, rank by
rank. Müller identified this khan, too, with the khan who brought
Manichaeism to the Uyghurs.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from the German of Le Coq and
Müller. Gaps are marked [ … ]; words they marked as uncertain are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense.

### Müller's T II D 135: the blessing on the khan, "the emanation of Mani"

*Back of a leaf from the opening of a Manichaean book; the text in black and
red. The beginning is lost.*

**First column.** [May the angels] with their own, the helpers, themselves
protect and guard the whole community of the hearers; first of all the
great ruler, the great majesty, the good, the blessed, worthy of the two
blessednesses, the two lives and the two lordships, of body and of soul;
the ruler of the East, the keeper of the religion and helper of the
righteous; the shining hearer, the wearer of the diadem, the ruler of
praised and blessed name, *Ulug Ilig Tängridä Qut Bulmïš Ärdämin Il Tutmïš
Alp Qutlug Külüg Bilgä*, khan of the Uyghurs, the emanation of Mani [ … ]

**Second column.** And also the [ … ] great protectors (?) and generals of
the blessed ruler. First of all the Il-Ügäsi Nigōšakpat (master of the
hearers), the Il-Ügäsi Yägän Säväg Tutuq, the Il-Ügäsi Ötür Buila Tarxan.
And also those (titled) *tutuq*: Tapmïš Qutlug Tutuq, Čüg Tutuq; and those
(titled) *čigši*: Waga Čigši, Yägän Öz Čigši, Tudun Čigši, [ … ] Čangši, Kül
Sangun Tiräk, Inanču Bilgä Tiräk. And also the *buila*s: Tapmïš Buila
Tarxan, Ašpädä (Ašpara?) Buila. May they all live unharmed, and at the end
may they receive the reward of the pious for ever. [So] may it be!

And further the palace officials of name, who do their service before the
pious ruler: first of all Ötür Buila Tarxan, Qutlug Ičräki [ … ], Tapmïš,
Ina[nču] [ … ], Qutlug [ … ] (the rest is lost).

## Notes to this edition

- **"The emanation of Mani"** (Middle Persian *zahag ī Mānī*): Müller
  explained *zahag* as "emanation, birth", a fitting honorific for the Turkish
  patron of the Manichaean church. He identified the khan with the one named
  on the Karabalgasun inscription who brought Manichaeism to the Uyghurs
  (the khan Mou-yü, reigned 759–780).
- **Bögü Khan and the year of the Sheep**: Le Coq noted that if the khan is
  Bögü, the year of the Sheep could be 767, and that the three *mahistaks*
  need not have been the first Manichaeans settled in the Uyghur state,
  since the khan is already on friendly terms with the *možak*.
- **Tonyuquq** was the adviser of the early Turkic khans, known from his
  own runic inscription.
- **The ranks** in T II D 135 are, after the ruler: the Il-Ügäsi, the
  *tutuq*, the *čigši*, the *tiräk* and the *buila*, then the *ičräki*
  (palace officials). Müller compared the different order in the preface of
  the [Mahrnāmag](mahrnamag.html); several titles are Chinese loanwords.
- Müller ended his article with a greeting to Thomsen "in the sounds of the
  Persian and Turkish followers of Mani": "unharmed, without sorrow, …
  for long years … always remain glad, live long!"
