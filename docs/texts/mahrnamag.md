## Introduction

M 1 is a nearly complete double leaf from a Manichaean hymnbook
(*mahrnāmag*, "book of hymns") in Middle Persian. The first leaf is the
preface: a long blessing on the reigning Uyghur khan, his family and his
court, with dozens of names and titles in Turkish, Sogdian, Persian and
Chinese, followed by an account of how the book came to be written. The
second leaf is part of the table of contents: the first lines of the
hymns, in alphabetical order, in four sections.

The account of the book's making is the most important part. It was begun
"in the year five hundred and forty-six after the birth of the Envoy of
Light", and in the year 162 after the death of Mār Šād Ohrmizd, but the
scribe could not finish it, and it lay unfinished "for many years" in a
building of the fortress until the preacher Yazd-āmad found it and had it
completed.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from F. W. K. Müller's German
of 1912. Gaps are marked [ … ]; words Müller marked as guesses are marked
(?); words in parentheses are added for sense. The table of contents gives
only the first words of each hymn, which end in "…" in the manuscript.

In the year five hundred and forty-six after the birth of the Envoy of

## Notes to this edition

- **The khan** of the preface bears the throne-name *Ai Tängridä Qut
  Bulmïš Alp Bilgä*. Müller distinguished him from the khan who first
  brought Manichaeism to the Uyghurs (Bögü Khan, who reigned 759–780 and
  is called "emanation of Mani" in another fragment, T II D 135); the khan
  here is called "the shining limb of the Envoy of Light".
- **The date.** Counting from Mani's birth in 216, the year 546 would be
  about 762, close to the time the Uyghur khan adopted Manichaeism. The
  year "after he was raised up in power" was left blank by the scribe. Mār
  Šād Ohrmizd was the leader of the eastern (Dēnāwar) Manichaeans, who
  died about 600.
- **Panjkand and Čīnānčkand**: Müller glosses Panjkand as "Pentapolis" (the
  five cities). Čīnānčkand, "the Chinese town", is usually identified with
  Qočo.
- **The table of contents**: within each section the hymns run
  alphabetically by the first letter in the Manichaean script, so the
  English order follows the manuscript, not the English alphabet.
- **"Teacher" (?)** (*kes*): in his addenda Müller says that in three places
  of the account of the book's making (his lines 174, 218 and 221) *kes* is
  better taken as "teacher"; the translation follows him.
- **Mār Zakū** (in the table of contents) was a disciple of Mani; Müller
  refers to al-Nadīm's lists.
- **Names.** The spellings of personal names follow Müller's readings, in
  simplified form. Many are uncertain, and modern editions read some of
  them differently.
