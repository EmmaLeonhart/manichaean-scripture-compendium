## Introduction

Ibn al-Nadīm, a Baghdad bookseller, finished his *Fihrist* ("Catalogue")
in 987. Its chapter on the Manichaeans, based on Manichaean sources and on
his own meetings with Manichaeans in Baghdad, is one of the main witnesses
to Mani's teaching. This page gives the part that lists Mani's books, the
chapters of the *Book of Mysteries*, and the titles of the letters "which
Mani and the leaders after him wrote". For most of the books, this list and
a few quotations are all that survive.

**This translation is a draft.** It is made from Gustav Flügel's German of
1862, which is in the public domain. Flügel's notes, which discuss the
readings of the Arabic names, are not reproduced. Words he marked as
uncertain are marked (?); "…" marks a title or chapter list left blank in
the Arabic.

## The translation

### The names of the books written by Mani

Mani wrote seven books, one in Persian and six in Syriac. At their head
stands:

**I. The Book of Mysteries**, which contains the following chapters:

1. On the Daisanites (the followers of Bardaisan).
2. On the testimony of Hystaspes against the Beloved.
3. On the testimony of Jacob against himself.
4. On the son of the poor widow, who in Mani's meaning is the crucified
   Messiah, whom the Jews crucified.
5. On the testimony of Jesus against himself, in relation to the Jews.
6. The beginning of the testimony of Yamīn after his victory.
7. On the seven spirits.
8. On the teaching of the four perishable spirits.
9. On laughter.
10. On the testimony of Adam about Jesus.
11. On falling away from the religion.
12. On the teaching of the Daisanites about the soul and the body.
13. A polemic against the Daisanites about the living soul.
14. On the three ditches.
15. On the preservation of the world.
16. On the three days.
17. On the prophets.
18. On the resurrection.

These are the chapters that the Book of Mysteries contains.

**II. The Book of the Giants**, containing …

**III. The Book of Precepts for the Hearers**, with a chapter of precepts
for the Elect (?).

**IV. The Book Šābuhragān.** This book contains the chapters: 1. On the
dissolution of the hearers. 2. On the dissolution of the Elect (?). 3. On
the dissolution of the sinners.

**V. The Book of Bringing to Life**, containing …

## Notes to this edition

- **Seven books, but six listed.** Al-Nadīm says Mani wrote seven books and
  names six; the *Gospel*, which he discusses elsewhere, is not in this
  list. Compare the list of seven books in the Chinese
  [Compendium](compendium.html).
- **The *Šābuhragān*** is the one book Mani wrote in Persian; its chapters
  on the "dissolution" (death and fate) of the hearers, the Elect and the
  sinners agree with the Turfan fragments on the end of the world
  ([Šābuhragān](shabuhragan.html)).
- **The letters.** The list includes letters by Mani's successors as well
  as his own. Müller matched the "Epistle of Hatā" quoted in the Turfan
  Middle Persian extracts with no. 65, the letter to Ḥaṭṭā (see [Mani's
  Gospel and Epistles](turfan-gospel-epistles.html)).
- **"The Elect (?)"** (books III and IV): Flügel's German has "the misled"
  (*Verführten*) in both places, which does not fit; the class meant beside
  the hearers and the sinners must be the Elect, and the translation gives
  that with a question mark.
- **Spellings.** The personal and place names are given as Flügel read
  them, simplified; many are uncertain in the Arabic.
