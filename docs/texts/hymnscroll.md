## Introduction

The *Xiabuzan* (下部讚, "Hymns for the Lower Section", that is, for the
hearers and the lay community) is the hymnbook of the Chinese Manichaean
church, preserved in one scroll from Dunhuang. Most of its hymns are in
lines of seven characters, translated from Parthian and Middle Persian
originals; some passages are Iranian hymns written phonetically in
Chinese characters, which are given here in transcription and not
translated.

**This translation is a draft, made for this compendium from the Chinese,
and it is in progress.** Each line below renders one couplet (two
seven-character lines) of the original. Square brackets [ … ] mark gaps in
the manuscript; parentheses ( ) mark words added for sense. Headings are
the scroll's own titles.

## The translation

### Opening (Iranian hymn in Chinese transcription)

The scroll begins with a damaged hymn in an Iranian language (it repeats
*wu-lu-shen* 烏嚧詵, Parthian *rōšn*, "light"), written in Chinese
characters for their sound. It is not translated.

### 1. Hymn in praise of Jesus (讚夷數文)

I worship and praise the ever-flourishing Tree, adorned with many jewels, wondrous beyond compare.  
Its trunk spreads out and fills the world; it saves the leaves and flowers [ … ].

All the buddhas come forth from among its flowers; all wisdom is born in its fruit.  
It can nourish the five kinds of sons of light; it can subdue the five kinds of greed [ … ].

The King of Mind is pure and ever watchful; to those who believe and wake, he gives more remembrance.  
If any go forward and stand firm, he leads them to rest on the level, right road.

Now I have had the eye of my buddha-nature opened, and can see the wondrous Dharma-body of the four places;  
I have had the ear of my buddha-nature opened too, and can hear the pure [voice] of the three constants.

So I make my heart clear and worship and praise, putting away every scattered thought, with true words.  
Before this, unknowing, I committed many faults; now I repent with all my heart, that my sins may be wiped away.

Ever-flourishing jewel Tree, ocean of life, in your compassion hear my true petition.  
Your names are countless, from land to land; your arts are countless, from land to land.

Compassionate Father of every light-nature, pitying Mother of all who have been carried off,  
save me now from the jackals and wolves, for you are the Jesus of Light [ … ].

The Great Holy One is himself an endless treasury, filled full with every kind of jewel;  
he opens it and gives to all the poor, and each obtains what his heart desires.

The Great Holy One is himself the second Venerable, and also the third, the one who can interpret;  
with his own pure company he proclaims the holy will [ … ] awakening.

He is also the eighth form of light, the guide, the one to lean on,  
the true face of all the buddhas, the king in the heart of all wisdom,

the true awakening of those adorned with jewels, the gate of liberation for those who do good works.  
For the plundered he is rescue; for the bound he can loose.

For the hard-pressed he is ease; for the afflicted he makes joy.  
He comforts all who mourn, and brings back to life every light-nature.

Now I beg you earnestly, grant my prayers: (take me) out of the poison-fire sea of the body of flesh,  
whose waves boil and surge without a moment's pause, where the makara goes in and out and swallows the ships.

It is at root the demons' palace, the land of the rākṣasas; it is also a thick forest, a marsh of reeds,  
where every evil bird and beast runs wild, and poisonous insects and vipers swarm.

It is also the body of the Demon of Greed, of evil deeds; it is also the many-formed [ … ].  
It is also the fivefold pit of the realm of darkness, and the court of the five poisons of ignorance;

the shoot of the three poisons without compassion, and the spring of the five poisons without wisdom;  
above and below, the wheel of the two poisons, cold and heat; the twice-seven of two kinds, the twelve palaces.

All the demon men and demon women are born and appear from the body of flesh.  
It is also the gate of the three worlds and the five paths, and the mouth of all the demons of the ten directions;

the dark mother of every demon king, the root and source of every evil deed.  
It is also the heart of the fierce, poisonous yakṣa, and the thought in the mind of the Demon of Greed;

the armour and weapons of every demon king, the poisoned net of all who break the teaching.  
It can sink the jewels and the merchant; it can hide the buddhas of light, the sun and moon.

It is the door of every hell, the road of every round of rebirth.  
In vain it shook the ever-abiding King of Nirvana; in the end it will be burned and shut in the eternal prison.

Now it still stands in my way: cangue, lock and fetter bind me round for ever.  
It makes me as if mad and as if drunk, so that I sin against the three constants and the body of the four places.

The grass and trees of the great earth, the stars of heaven, the dust and sand of the great earth and the fine rain:  
the faults I have committed are thousands and thousands of times more than these.

Jesus Buddha, adorned with broad wisdom, rouse your great compassion and forgive my sins;  
hear my words of such pain, and lead me out of this poison-fire sea.

Grant me the incense of the precepts and the water of liberation, the twelve jewelled crowns, robes and necklaces;  
wash my wondrous nature free of dust, adorn my pure body and make it fair.

Take away the knots of the three poisons of the three winters, and the six poison winds of the six thieves;  
send down the great spring of the Law, to make the ground of my nature flourish and the flowers and fruit of the tree of my nature grow rich.

Calm the great waves of the sea of fire, the dark clouds and dark mists, all that binds and covers;  
send down the great sun of the Law shining everywhere, to make my heart and nature ever bright and pure.

Take away the sickness of folly of many kalpas, and the goblins and all the demons;  
send down the great medicine of the Law to heal me quickly, and drive them off with holy spells.

I suffer so many hindrances as these, and countless other hardships besides;  
Great Holy One, look on me and have pity; save me, let no more disasters trouble me.

I pray only that Jesus send down compassion and loose me from the bonds of the demons;  
now I am in the pit of fire: lead me quickly to rest in the pure land.

Great King of Physicians to all the sick, great radiance to all in darkness,  
you gather in all that is scattered, and to the hearts of the gods [ … ].

I am dead now: I pray you, bring me back to life. I am darkened now: I pray you, give me light.  
The demon king scattered me through the ten directions, and drew me to follow forms and be stained by the three existences;

he made me dazed and drunk and senseless, so that I sinned against the three constants and the body of the four places.  
Ignorance and foolish love twine about me always: send down the great medicine of the Law to cure me.

Great Holy One, stretch out your hand of compassion quickly, and lay it on the bright crown of my buddha-nature.  
Guard me always, at every hour; let not the demons' party come to harm me.

Give me back the joy I had before in my own realm; wash away the afflictions of my countless kalpas.  
Make whole the wondrous adornment of my light-nature, as it was before it sank into the realm of desire.

Open again the pure, wondrous radiance, the new pure land adorned with many jewels,  
the new sun of wisdom, blue as lapis, shining on the pure and wondrous land of my Dharma-body.

The Great Holy One is himself the season of blessing, shining on all of us light-natures;  
his wondrous form has no equal in the world, and so it is with his powers and transformations.

Now he appears in the wondrous form of a boy, and drives mad the five kinds of female demons;  
now in the fair body of a girl, and throws into frenzy the five kinds of [ … ].

He is the compassionate Son of the Venerable of Light, and the Father who can save the light-nature;  
the highest Brother of all the buddhas, and the Mother of wisdom and compassion.

### 2. Hymn in praise of Jesus, second part (讚夷數文 第二疊)

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: compassionate and true Father, whose whole face is mercy,  
forgive the faults I have committed, and take me away from the demons' house and its false kindness.

Supreme Venerable of Light, power within power; supreme ambrosia, king within wisdom,  
you give all beings the wish-granting jewel: receive me and lead me out of this deep sea of fire.

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: you who save from suffering and judge fairly, without regard for persons,  
spread wide, I beg you, your wings of compassion, and take me away from the demon birds that trample.

The kin of the senseless body of flesh are all children of the deep pit;  
the demonic natures that hem me in, within and without, harm my pure body all the time.

No evil beast can match them; what poisonous snake is like them?  
They are like the cutting wind and frost at the end of autumn, which strip [the good … ].

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: King of Physicians, fine in works and complete in wisdom,  
the good friend, by whom the sick are healed; the kind and pitying one, by whom we meet with joy.

The natures of bodies, bounded and unbounded, have long been hurt and sunk in the sea of birth and death,  
their limbs scattered through the three worlds: gather them, I pray, and raise them back above all that exists.

Let not the stream of the true Law be cut off again; let us not be thrown again into the demons' mouth.  
Send down the power of great means and compassion, and bring back to life, I pray, all the light-natures in distress.

Let them not be carried off again by the demon army; let not the enemy come again to kill them.  
Cover me with your wings of light and compassion, and forgive the sins of my nature in both its bodies.

I pray only: send down your hand of great compassion and lay it on my threefold pure Dharma-body;  
clear away the bonds of countless kalpas, and wash off the dust and dirt of countless kalpas.

Open the eye of light of my Law-nature, so that I may see unhindered the body of the four places;  
seeing unhindered the body of the four places, I shall escape the many hardships of four kinds.

Open the ear of light of my Law-nature, so that I may hear unhindered the sound of the wondrous Law;  
hearing unhindered the sound of the wondrous Law, I shall escape every kind of falsehood and crookedness.

Open the mouth of light of my Law-nature, so that I may praise in full the three constants and the fourfold Dharma-body;  
praising in full the three constants and the fourfold Dharma-body, I shall escape the hymns of a muddled, mixed mind.

Open the hands of light of my Law-nature, so that I may touch all over the suchness of the four tranquil bodies;  
touching all over the suchness of the four tranquil bodies, I shall escape sinking in the calamity of the four elements.

Loose my feet, fettered for many years, so that I may walk the true road of the three constants;  
walking the true road of the three constants, I shall come quickly to the land of peace and joy.

Bring me back to the heart of true suchness, pure and bright, ever at rest;  
pure and bright and ever at rest, I shall leave for ever all the inversions of delusion.

Grant that I may always see the compassionate Father, and never again suffer the round of birth and death.  
My senses purified and my heart awakened, may I never again be dazed and unaware.

Now I rely on the Great Holy Venerable: may I never again sink and stray on the road of birth and death.  
Send down quickly your hand of light and compassion; never again cast me away [into the demons' mouth].

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: send down great compassion and shield me always;  
forgive my faults of countless kalpas, as (you forgave) the men and women of the past.

I am the bright lamb of the Great Holy One; with tears and weeping I tell my wrong:  
suddenly the jackals, wolves and fierce beasts carried me off from the good shepherd of light.

Send down great compassion and gather me up, I beg you; let me into the gentle flock of light,  
that I may reach the fair peaks and forests of the mountain of the Law, and roam free and ever unafraid.

I am also the bright seed-grain of the Great Holy One, thrown among thick forest and thorns;  
send down great compassion and gather me up, I beg you, into the bright granary on the threshing-floor of the Law.

I am also the vine-branch of the Great Holy One, first planted in the pure garden of the Law;  
suddenly creepers sprang up and wound round me, drawing off my wondrous strength and making me wither.

I am also the rich soil of the Great Holy One, in which the demons sowed the trees of the five poisons;  
I hope only for the sharp hoe, knife and sickle of the Law, to cut them down and burn them and make it clean.

The other evil weeds and thorns: may the fire of the precepts clear them all away,  
so that the fifteen kinds of shoots may flourish, and the fifteen [ … ] spread out.

I am also the new and wondrous robe of the Great Holy One, suddenly fouled by the demons' dust;  
I hope only for the water of the Law to wash it fresh, so that I may reach the pure body of the Dharma-body.

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: Tree of Life, adorned with many jewels,  
highest and peerless wondrous King of Physicians, peace and pure works, complete in every good.

Ever-flourishing jewel Tree, ocean of life: its base is firm, a body of adamant;  
its trunk is truth, without false words; its branches are long and great, ever joyful;

its leaves of compassion are full of every jewel; its fruit of ambrosia is ever fresh and never withers.  
Those who eat it are cut off for ever from the stream of birth and death; its sweet fragrance fills the world.

Great Holy One, already perfect, may you live long: you can bring back to life the ever-flourishing tree of the Law-nature.  
Wisdom, pure and empty, ever watchful: its fruit is the King of Mind, who discerns with skill.

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: King of the Law of Complete Wisdom, Jesus Buddha,  
keep my body of flesh ever well, keep my buddha-nature free of entanglement and stain.

At every hour increase my remembrance, and take me away from the devouring mouths of the demons;  
take me away from the devouring mouths of the demons, and keep me for ever apart from evil deeds and greed [ … ].

Let me into the wondrous forest of the Law, with its sweet flowers; let me into the pure and gentle flock of lambs.  
Keep the foundation of my faith ever firm, and let me be found worthy of praise.

I cry out earnestly in sorrow and pray with a true heart: compassionate Father, King of the Law, Lord of life,  
you can save my nature from disaster, you can make my pure body ever joyful.

You give ease, you save from suffering, you are compassion, you forgive faults:  
give my light-nature encouragement and delight, give my pure body a place to rest.

You can break the mountain of knives and the forest of swords, you can subdue the lion and silence the vipers;  
you can cure every sickness that is hard to cure, and part us from the attachments that are hard to give up.

Now I hold firmly to the gate of the Law; Great Holy One, in your compassion shield me always.  
With all my heart I praise the name of the compassionate Father: with deep reverence, may it be so in the end.

### 3. Lament on impermanence (歎無常文)

*Composed by Mār Sīsin, King of the Law, when he was oppressed by a tyrant.*

I tell all of you who are wise: each of you, hear the true words of life.  
Mani Buddha, the King of the Law of Complete Wisdom, has made all of it plain, as if before your eyes.

Since we have been awakened by the Great Holy One, we must give up all love and attachment,  
set our hearts firmly in the gate of the true Law, and seek nirvana diligently, crossing the great sea.

I tell also the people of high rank and good fortune, who seek liberation with a single mind:  
strive and practise, give yourselves no idle moment, and leave quickly every fear of birth and death.

No world abides for ever, and nothing one leans on is real either;  
like the phantom city and towers in the desert, fools rush after them and lose their lives.

The glory and rank of the world, those who are free by a little merit,  
are like clouds rising over the mountains on four sides: they gather, the wind blows on them, and they quickly scatter.

The stinking, filthy body of flesh does not last; when the time of impermanence comes, all of it is broken,  
like the flowers and leaves of spring, briefly flourishing on the branch: how can they stay firm and ever green?

The one who made the body of flesh was a skilled craftsman: he was the false and evil Demon King.  
When he had finished such a den, he netted the light-nature and hid himself.

The thankless fire of hunger makes a chain; it kills living beings without stopping;  
all day it eats the parts of their bodies, and still it does not escape the suffering of birth and death.

All the riches one heaps up come from evil deeds and false words;  
on the day of impermanence they are all left behind, and still they serve as fetters for the light-nature.

First cut off the desires of ignorance, love and attachment: they are the sea of all affliction;  
in the future, because of them, one suffers every disaster; in this life they serve as shackles for the buddha-nature.

Alas for the beings of the world! They cannot believe truly and seek the right road;  
day and night they seek wealth without pause, all for the body of flesh, whose master is the Demon of Greed.

When the body of flesh is destroyed, the demon goes out, and the sinful deeds bring disaster on the pure nature;  
wherever it is born, it suffers disasters, truly because the deeds of its former body were not right.

The body of flesh you love and cherish must be given up in the end; all that is born goes back to extinction.  
All your riches, fields and houses: though you mean not to give them up, you part from them in the end.

Though you win glory in the world, break your heart and loathe the suffering of birth and death;  
give up pride and wrongdoing, and practise with a single mind on the road to nirvana.

At birth one is naked, and at death the same; however much one heaps up, it does not last.  
Sons and daughters, wives, concubines and finery all stay behind after death, to serve another master.

Alone one goes, carrying only shame and evil deeds; after death one bears them on one's back.  
Before the King of Equal Justice all are found in the wrong, and are sent back to the suffering of the round of birth and death.

Again they are held fast by the Demon King; meeting no good conditions, they grow more and more defiled.  
Some go into hell, some are burned, some are shut up with the demons in the eternal prison.

Singing, dancing, laughter and every kind of music, eating a hundred flavours, managing fields and houses:  
all are like things seen in a dream, gone when you wake. Think it over carefully: there is nothing to lean on.

Your kin in this world, for a little while: how are they different from guests lodging at an inn?  
At dusk they all stay the night together; at dawn they part and go back to their own countries.

Wives, concubines, sons and daughters are like creditors: all of it comes from the harm done to one another in the past.  
All of them are enemies and thieves of compassion, and so one's heart is divided, paying them back their strength.

The bodies of beings who eat flesh are like tombs, and no different from bottomless pits:  
they wrongly kill countless kinds of living things, to feed the soldiers of the three poisons and the six thieves.

The buddha-nature, still and clear, is shut up inside; pressed by the afflictions, it suffers always.  
Greed, lust, the fire of hunger and disasters from before: there is not a moment when they do not boil it.

The world grows worse and is always in haste; high and low lord it over one another, and there is no joy.  
Beings only grow poorer and more wretched; the rich are driven by the demons without rest.

Those who practise good are very few; those who do evil are without number.  
The demon of greed, lust and gluttony is a blazing king: even if beings meet good conditions, they fall back and scatter.

Face to face they vie with fine words, with no shame or fear at all;  
against the great power and wisdom of the holy light, they heap up false words they have no right to.

Beings are mostly covered by ignorance, and will not practise the true road diligently;  
they slander the Buddha, revile the Law and despise the true monks, and only do harm and never protect.

You who are wise, look closely: by whom were the great world and the small world made?  
When they were set up, why were they made? You must understand both the harm and the good.

All sentient beings, of every form and kind, and the places where the world's making and unmaking puts them:  
all these are secret matters. In the end, where do the ten thousand things go back to?

Mani of good works has opened it all and made it known, revealing all natures and forms.  
You who seek liberation must awaken, understand and think carefully.

Give alms, keep the fast, read and recite diligently, discern with wisdom and receive the pure precepts;  
have pity and fear, and good conduct: by this power you will escape disaster and distress.

Leap with joy and stand firm in the true Law; practise wisdom diligently and live by the Law;  
together give up all evil conduct, and set your hearts firmly on the place of liberation.

Better to be free now, for the nature's sake, and give up all the habits of love and desire:  
when impermanence suddenly comes and presses on you, regret in that hour comes too late.

Think carefully: in all the world there is not one thing that can be relied on.  
Kin, sons and daughters, wives and concubines will not take your place on the day of impermanence.

Only the two kinds of deeds, good and evil, go with the buddha-nature, walking and sitting.  
All glory, treasures and playthings must be given up on the day of impermanence.

The wise see it and practise beforehand, and are not overrun by the Demon King and by birth and death.  
Those who can give up love and all glory and pleasure escape sinking in the three poisons and the five desires.

I pray that all, with one heart, may climb the right road and quickly gain the pure land of nirvana,  
where the seven calamities and the four sufferings never were: so it is called the ever-joyful [land].

## Notes to this edition

- **Jesus** (夷數) in these hymns is the divine Jesus, the "Jesus of
  Light" who awakens the soul, called here King of Physicians and the Tree
  of Life.
- **"The three constants and the four places"** (三常, 四處): Manichaean
  formulas for the divine realm. The Chinese texts use them often without
  explaining them.
- **"The twice-seven of two kinds, the twelve palaces"** (二七兩般十二殿,
  hymn 1) probably refers to the planets and the twelve signs of the
  zodiac, the "palaces" of the heavens, which the Manichaeans counted
  among the demons' works. The reading is uncertain.
- **"The many-formed [ … ]"** (多形卑訢期, hymn 1): the last three
  characters look like a transcribed foreign word and are not translated.
- **"Both its bodies"** (雨般, hymn 2): 雨 is read as 兩, "two kinds".
- **Mār Sīsin** (末思信, hymn 3) was Mani's successor as head of the
  church; Manichaean sources say he was put to death under Bahrām II.
- **"Mani of good works"** (善業忙儞) and **"Mani Buddha"** (忙儞佛) use a
  different spelling of Mani's name (忙儞) from the *Compendium* (摩尼).
