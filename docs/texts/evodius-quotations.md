## Introduction

Evodius, bishop of Uzalis in North Africa and a friend of Augustine, wrote
*On the Faith, against the Manichaeans* about the year 400. Like Augustine,
he quotes the Latin Manichaean books that circulated in Africa, and some of
his quotations are not found elsewhere: a sentence from the second book of
Mani's *Treasure of Life*, a fuller text of the Fundamental Epistle on the
fate of the souls that loved the world, and the Epistle's teaching that the
one crucified was not the Saviour but the Prince of Darkness.

Evodius also quotes, word for word, the long passage of the seventh book of
the *Treasure* on the "ships of light", which Augustine quotes too; it is on
the [Fundamental Epistle page](fundamental-epistle.html) and is not repeated
here.

**This translation is new and is a draft.** It is made directly from the
Latin of Zycha's edition (1892), read from an OCR scan and corrected by
sense; the Latin is itself a translation from Mani's Syriac. Evodius'
surrounding arguments are summarized in italics. Words in parentheses are
added for sense; [ … ] marks where Evodius breaks off with "and so on".

## The translation

### From the *Treasure of Life*, book II

*Evodius argues that even Mani had to admit that souls sin by their own
will: "for so he says in the second book of the Treasure":*

But those who, through their own negligence, have by no means allowed
themselves to be cleansed from the stain of the aforesaid spirits, and have
obeyed the divine commandments too little and not wholly, and have been
unwilling to keep more fully the law given them by their liberator, nor have
governed themselves as was fitting [ … ]

### From the Fundamental Epistle: the souls that loved the world

*"Again, in the Fundamental Epistle he says this of those souls":*

Those (souls) which, through love of the world, have allowed themselves to
stray from their former shining nature, and have become enemies of the holy
light, and have openly armed themselves for the destruction of the holy
elements, and have followed the fiery spirit, and with their hostile
persecution have also afflicted the holy church and the Elect set in it,
the keepers of the heavenly commandments, are kept away from the blessedness
and glory of the holy land. And because they have allowed themselves to be
overcome by evil, they will remain in that same stock of evil, that
peaceful land and the immortal regions being forbidden to them. This will
happen to them because they have so bound themselves with wicked works that
they have been made strangers to the life and freedom of the holy light.
They will not, then, be able to be received into those peaceful kingdoms,
but will be fixed in the horrible globe spoken of before, over which a
guard must also be set. So those same souls will cling to the things they
have loved, being left in that same globe of darkness, winning this for
themselves by their own deserts. For they did not care to learn of these
things to come, nor did they separate themselves from them when time was
given.

*Evodius counts the phrases that show the will: "through their own
negligence", "have by no means allowed", "have obeyed too little", "have
been unwilling to keep", "have followed", "have allowed themselves to be
overcome", "have bound themselves", "winning this for themselves by their
own deserts", "they did not care to learn … nor did they separate
themselves". He adds that "in the same epistle" Mani speaks of*

the cut-off part of the light, that is, the sinful souls.

### From the Fundamental Epistle: the kingdoms of light

*Evodius quotes Mani's words that God is*

abounding in all good things, nothing being lacking or set lowest among the
splendours of his kingdoms; and his most splendid worlds are so founded
that they can never be shaken or moved by anyone.

*and that "a little later" he adds:*

But the Father of the most blessed light, knowing that a great ruin and
desolation, which would rise out of the darkness, threatened his holy
worlds, unless he set against it some outstanding and renowned deity,
mighty in strength, by which he might at once overcome and destroy the
stock of darkness, so that, when it was put out, rest might be made ready
for the dwellers in the light [ … ]

*Evodius goes on to report two further passages without quoting them in
full. At the end of the same epistle, he says, Mani writes that the part of
God that has been mixed (with darkness) cannot all be called back to its
former freedom. And in the first book of the* Treasure*, he says, it is
written that the divine substance which Mani speaks of "is today subject to
the race of darkness, as clay to the potter".*

### From the Fundamental Epistle: the crucifixion

*Evodius answers the Manichaean question "What would the Jews have done to
Christ, if he had not wished to suffer at their hands?" by pointing out that,
by Mani's own teaching, Christ had no flesh and suffered nothing from the
Jews. "For so he says in the Fundamental Epistle":*

For the enemy, who hoped that he had crucified the same Saviour, the father
of the righteous, was himself crucified; at that time one thing was done and
another shown. So the Prince of Darkness was fastened to the cross, and he
it was who wore the crown of thorns, with his companions, and had the
scarlet robe; he also drank the vinegar and gall which some have thought
the Lord drank. And all that he (the Saviour) seemed to endure here was laid
upon the leaders of the darkness, who were also wounded by the nails and
the lance.

## Notes to this edition

- **"The horrible globe"** (Latin *globus*, Greek *bōlos*) is the mass in
  which, at the end, the darkness and the souls that loved it are shut up
  for ever. Compare the "grave … closed with a stone as big as the world" in
  [al-Nadīm](al-nadim-teaching.html).
- **"The cut-off part of the light"** (*lucis succisiva pars*): Evodius gives
  only these words, with Mani's gloss "that is, the sinful souls".
- **The crucifixion passage** is the Manichaean "docetic" teaching that the
  Saviour did not suffer in a body of flesh; compare the Middle Persian
  retelling of the Passion in [the Turfan fragments](turfan-passion.html),
  which follows the Gospels closely.
- **Evodius' text** also quotes the Acts of the apostles "written by
  Leucius, which (the Manichaeans) accept", on "beautiful figments, feigned
  show and the compulsion of visible things"; that is not by Mani and is not
  given here.
